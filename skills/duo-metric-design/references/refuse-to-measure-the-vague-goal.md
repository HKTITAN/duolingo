# Refuse to Measure the Vague Goal

## Concept

Users state their goal in a word that cannot be falsified. Every team's instinct is to invent a score for that word anyway, because the word is what appears in the marketing. The result is a number nobody can argue with and nobody can improve against — it cannot be wrong, so it cannot tell you anything.

The move is to refuse. Say publicly that the headline word is not measurable, decompose it into named sub-competencies that are, and report against those. Each sub-score becomes a scoreboard entry that tells you which specific investment moved which specific capability. It also lets you be honest with users about what you do and do not deliver.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/can-duolingo-make-me-fluent (Duolingo blog, 2024-11-18; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo **publicly declines to measure "fluency"**, calling it a vague goal that language experts themselves shy away from — despite it being the exact word most users would use for what they want.
- Instead it assesses across **four named domains — speaking, writing, reading, listening** — mapped onto external CEFR proficiency levels, so the scale is not one Duolingo invented for itself.
- Reported against a legible external yardstick: learners completing beginner content in Spanish and French scored **comparably to four semesters of university study** on reading and listening. About **50% of learners hit on-target speaking scores** — a number that is visibly weaker than the reading and listening results and is published anyway. Scope: **100+ courses in 40+ languages**.
- Stated tension: the honest answer to "can this make me fluent" becomes **"it's up to you and how you use it"** — truthful, and commercially weaker than a competitor who simply claims fluency.

Two supporting disciplines. First, ship raw artifacts next to the scores: after the graded adaptive portion of the Duolingo English Test, takers complete an **ungraded video interview and writing sample that are shared alongside their scores** to universities — unscored evidence traveling with the number so the recipient can judge for themselves. Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-english-test-readiness (Duolingo blog, 2021-10-14; accessed 2026-09-22)

Second, audit your decomposition for staleness. In Duolingo's France report, **"other" was the second-largest stated motivation at ~16%, ahead of "just for fun" at ~8%, and 18% of French teenagers chose it**. Investigating that bucket found young learners studying for fandoms and pop culture — a real segment that fell outside the school/work/travel taxonomy entirely. Context: **52% of French Duolingo users are 13–29**, and France grew **+134% since 2020**. Source: blog.duolingo.com/what-languages-do-they-study-in-france (Duolingo blog, 2024-03-11; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

1. **Find the word in your positioning that nobody can define.** Ask three colleagues to define it independently. If the answers differ, it is not a metric.
2. **Decompose it into 3–5 named capabilities** that can each be scored on their own and that a user would recognize as distinct.
3. **Anchor to an external scale where one exists.** An outside yardstick is harder to quietly redefine when the numbers get uncomfortable.
4. **Publish the weak sub-score too.** A decomposition that only ever shows the strong dimensions is just the vague word with extra steps.
5. **Attach raw, unscored evidence.** Compression to a scalar destroys information the recipient may need and invites over-trust. The artifact keeps the score useful as a filter without letting it become the whole verdict.
6. **Watch your "other" bucket.** When it outranks named categories, the taxonomy has drifted from reality — and because it is unlabeled, it is invisible to every analysis that groups by category.

## Apply to your product

- What is the one word in your pitch that your team cannot define the same way twice, and what 3–5 things does it actually decompose into?
- Which of those sub-scores would embarrass you if published — and what happens to trust if a user finds it out some other way?
- What raw artifact could travel with your score so the recipient can check your compression?
