# Silhouette First, Reproducible From Primitives

## Concept

At the size a mark is actually seen — a tab bar, a notification, a favicon, a thumbnail — the outline is nearly all the information that survives. Interior detail cannot rescue a shape that does not read; it competes with it. So silhouette is the only variable worth months of iteration, and the way to make months of iteration converge rather than drift is to declare the invariants up front. A second property is worth designing for deliberately: if the mark can be rebuilt from simple primitives, ordinary people can draw it, and drawings get posted.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/reshaping-duo (Duolingo blog, 2013-11-20; accessed 2026-09-22)

- For the 2013 redesign the team declared Duo's **color and species non-negotiable** and everything cosmetic in play, then iterated the shape and silhouette **for months**.
- The loop was public and fast: sketches shown to the whole team **daily**, each round either resketched or promoted to vector.
- Movement and a **slight quarter-turn angle** were added only after the shape was settled, not as a way to fix a shape that was not working.
- Rollout went iPhone and iPad first, then web and other platforms.
- This is the 2013 process and the mark has continued to change since, so the durable part is the method, not that specific outline.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-to-draw-duo-the-owl (Duolingo blog, 2022-06-10; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo published a **10-step** guide to drawing Duo built entirely from primitives — a circle, a wavy shape, fin-shaped wings, pill-shaped feet, ovals for eyes, half-circles for the beak — with a call to post results under a fan-art hashtag. Duo had been the mascot for **10 years** at the time of writing.
- The guide instructs deliberate imperfection. Draw the left eye "just a tiny bit smaller" because the character sits on an angle, and do not line the beak halves up perfectly so the top sticks out on the left — both stated as being for perspective.
- Feet are drawn as **detached pill shapes floating below the body**, named as a house convention where elements sometimes float above or below a figure.
- Tension: reproducibility cuts both ways. A mark simple enough for a fan to draw is simple enough for anyone to imitate, parody or counterfeit, and the same guide teaches both uses.

## The transferable pattern

Sequence the work like this:

1. **Declare invariants before the first sketch.** Name the two or three properties that cannot change and put everything else in play. Without that list, long iteration turns into drift and the final round looks nothing like the brief.
2. **Iterate the outline only, at final size.** Judge every round as a filled black shape at the smallest size it will ship at. Detail, shading and motion come after the shape wins, never as a rescue.
3. **Review on a short, visible cadence.** Daily exposure to the whole team surfaces the "that reads as something else" reaction early, when a round is still cheap to throw away.
4. **Build from primitives on purpose.** Composing from circles, ovals and pill shapes lowers the execution floor enough that amateur attempts succeed, and a successful attempt gets shared. Publishing the construction recipe converts a brand asset into distribution you did not buy.
5. **Keep the small asymmetries.** Slight size mismatches and an element that does not quite line up are the perspective cues that make a form read as occupying space. Cleaning them up produces a technically neater drawing that reads flatter.

## Apply to your product

- Render your mark as a solid black shape at your smallest real size. Is it still identifiable, and is it distinguishable from the two competitors next to it in a list?
- What are the two properties of your mark that you would refuse to change, and has anyone on the team ever written them down?
- Could a person with no design tools reproduce a recognizable version of your mark in ten steps? If not, what is the cheapest simplification that would get you there?
