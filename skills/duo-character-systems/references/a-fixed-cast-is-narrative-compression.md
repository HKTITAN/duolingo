# A Fixed Cast Is Narrative Compression

## Concept

A recurring character with a strong, fixed personality carries its own motivation. Once the audience knows who this is, you spend none of a constrained budget re-establishing why they are behaving this way. That budget takes different forms — words available to a beginner, seconds of an onboarding video, pixels in a notification, tokens in a summary — but in all of them, recognition substitutes for exposition. The catch is that the compression only works if the personalities are actually fixed and actually understood, including by your own team.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/character-voices (Duolingo blog, 2021-08-23; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo spent months developing personalities, backstories and relationships for a cast of **nine characters** before using them.
- The payoff appears where the constraints are hardest. Stories are written under hard limits on length, vocabulary and grammar, and the post states the mechanism directly: "Instead of having to explain Lily's motivations in each story, her blithe, unamused avatar gives our learners a nuanced understanding."
- Voice casting was done in **English first**, then used as a blueprint for **Spanish, French, German and Japanese** — the personality is the spec, and each new language matches the spec rather than re-deciding who the character is.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/building-character (Duolingo blog, 2020-11-10; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo explored **vikings and space aliens** for the cast and rejected both as too far removed from the reality of language learning, settling on modern humans with very quirky personalities. Their framing: teaching someone to order food is the useful part, and what happens after they order is where the magic happens.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/designing-new-characters-internship (Duolingo blog, 2023-04-14; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The character Vikram was diagnosed as much less understood by audiences **and by Duolingo's own art team**. The fix was not to redraw him. The team designed his apartment — blueprint, furniture, kitchen and a rooftop garden built around his baking and gardening traits, with colors chosen not to clash so he stays visually dominant in the space.
- The team began generating content ideas off the initial sketches alone, which is the actual test that the characterization landed.
- Tension: a fixed cast is a commitment. Personalities that compress well are rigid by design, and as this case shows, a character can be locked into the product long before anyone notices it is not reading.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules for building a cast that pays for itself:

1. **Ground the fiction in the real task, exaggerate only the personality.** Quirk buys attention; distance costs relevance. If the scenario drifts far from what the user is actually doing, they are being entertained instead of served, and the two purposes stop reinforcing each other.
2. **Treat the personality sheet as a spec, not as flavor.** Everything downstream — new surfaces, new locales, new media, a voice, a tone in a notification — matches the spec instead of relitigating who this is. That is what keeps a cast consistent across teams.
3. **When a character reads flat, design its surroundings rather than redrawing it.** People infer traits from possessions, space and context far more readily than from appearance, and a built environment gives writers concrete hooks. The signal that it worked is that other people start generating ideas from it unprompted.

## Apply to your product

- Where in your product is the content budget hardest — a notification, an empty state, a fifteen-second video — and what are you currently spending it on that a recognized persona would carry for free?
- Are your personas written down as behavior specs that a new writer or a vendor could match, or do they live in one person's head?
- Pick the persona your team understands least. What would its workspace, tools or calendar look like, and does sketching that generate ideas the description never did?
