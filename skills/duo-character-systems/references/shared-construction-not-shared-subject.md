# Shared Construction, Not Shared Subject

## Concept

Teams usually try to keep a visual world coherent by controlling subject matter — same category of thing, same palette, same illustrator. That does not scale, and it is not what viewers actually read. People infer "same world" from repeated formal properties: proportion, shape language, eye scale, silhouette, how parts connect. Extract those properties into explicit rules and two useful things follow. Wildly different subjects can share a universe. And every future addition — a new character, a background figure, a vendor's asset, a user's avatar — inherits the coherence automatically instead of being negotiated one at a time.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/building-character (Duolingo blog, 2020-11-10; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duo was decomposed into **four explicit components**: simple geometric construction (essentially a body with wings), oversized endearing eyes, a distinctive body shape, and detached feet.
- Those components were then applied to a cast of human characters, so a culturally diverse human cast and a green owl read as inhabitants of the same world without sharing a species.
- Tension, stated in the post: they did not get it right the first time. **18 months** of countless designs, workshops, scrapped directions and consultations with outside professionals. This is not a sprint-sized investment and cannot be faked as one.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/designing-new-characters-internship (Duolingo blog, 2023-04-14; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Outside vendors were designing background characters individually for each scene, with very minimal style guidance beyond the main cast's character sheets — expensive, inconsistent, and liable to pull attention.
- The fix was a reusable **library of diverse background characters** built from the same simple geometric building blocks: interesting silhouettes, but explicitly not so wild as to distract from the main cast's performances. One artifact solved per-project cost and visual hierarchy at the same time.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/avatar-creator (Duolingo blog, 2023-07-19; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The avatar creator lets users customize hairstyle, body shape, piercings, skin tone and accessories, yielding **billions of possible combinations** — every one of them house-styled by construction, so there is no moderation surface at all.
- Tension: the switch is **one-way**. Once a profile is changed to an avatar, the user can no longer upload a photo, and no reason is given. An irreversible identity change is a real cost to anyone who tries it and wants out.

## The transferable pattern

Write the construction rules down as rules, not as examples. A page of reference images is not a system; "oversized eyes at roughly a third of head height, limbs that terminate in detached rounded forms, no sharp corners anywhere" is. The test is whether someone who has never seen your work can produce something that belongs.

Then use the same rule set three ways:

- **Extension.** New members of the cast are derived from the rules, so adding to the world does not dilute it.
- **Delegation.** Vendors and other teams get a pre-approved parts library rather than a brief. Deliberately design the shared parts to be less prominent than your hero elements, so delegated work fills in the background instead of competing with the foreground.
- **Self-expression.** A finite, house-styled parts library recombines into a space large enough that users feel individual, while every possible output is on-brand and safe. Free-form uploads buy the same feeling and commit you to moderation forever. If you do offer this, make the switch reversible — an identity change users cannot undo is a support burden and a trust cost.

## Apply to your product

- If a contractor who has never seen your product had to add one new element to your visual world tomorrow, what document would you hand them, and would it be rules or just pictures?
- Which formal properties do your existing assets actually share? If you cannot name three, your coherence is currently held together by one person's taste.
- Where do users want to express themselves in your product, and could a finite parts library give them that without opening a moderation queue?
