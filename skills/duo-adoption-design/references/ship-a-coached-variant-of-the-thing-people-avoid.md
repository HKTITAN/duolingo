# Ship a Coached Variant of the Thing People Avoid

## Concept

An open-ended surface asks the user to supply both the content and the courage. For a task people already dread, that is two asks too many. A coached variant supplies the content — it leads, it proposes the next move, it tells you how you did in terms you already understand — so the user only has to supply the courage, and then less of it. The coached version is a ramp, not a destination. Its job is to get the first attempt to happen.

## What Duolingo does

- Duolingo added **Video Call with Falstaff**, a *guided* counterpart to the existing *unguided* Video Call with Lily. Falstaff asks questions calibrated to the learner's level, suggests phrases and translations mid-call, and gives real-time feedback **in the learner's own language** rather than the one being practised.
- The stated plan is structural, not a one-off: **a guided and an unguided call in every unit**. Guided is a permanent parallel track, not a beginner detour that disappears.
- Launch scope: **January 2026**, **Duolingo Max subscribers only**, **iOS only**, **9 languages at launch**, with expansion across all Duolingo Scores targeted by end of year.
- Tension, and it is the important part: the feature built for the least confident users launched behind the **top subscription tier** on **one platform**. The people it was designed for are the people least likely to have bought the top tier. A ramp behind a paywall is a ramp most of its intended users never reach.
- Compare the opposite call elsewhere in the same product: the on-demand practice surface and per-answer explanations were both moved from paid to free, precisely because they serve the users who have not committed yet.

## The transferable pattern

Avoidance of a high-anxiety task is a starting problem, not a capability problem. Treat it as one.

The coached variant needs four properties:

1. **It leads.** The system proposes; the user responds. Never a blank surface.
2. **It is calibrated.** The prompts match where the user actually is, or the coaching itself becomes an exposure event.
3. **It gives feedback in the user's own frame of reference,** not in the jargon of the skill being acquired. Feedback the user has to decode is not feedback.
4. **It sits beside the open-ended version permanently,** at every stage — not only at onboarding. Confidence is not monotonic; users drop back down after a gap.

The packaging decision matters as much as the feature. If you gate the ramp behind the tier that only committed users buy, you have built an activation feature and sold it exclusively to the already-activated. Free or default for the coached variant; charge for the depth behind it.

## Apply to your product

- Which feature do users open and then close without acting? Write down whether they lacked ability or lacked a first move.
- What would a led version look like — the system proposes the first step, suggests the phrasing, and reports back in plain terms?
- Check your pricing table: is the ramp behind the tier only committed users buy? If so, you have priced out the exact segment it was built for.
