# Make Your Metric the Standard

## Concept

A proprietary score that means nothing outside your app is a vanity number — users chase it while they are engaged and abandon it the moment they are not. Putting that score on the same scale as a credential the outside world already recognizes converts it into a forecast of a real-world outcome, which is what makes it worth chasing at all. It also routes qualified demand into whatever paid assessment sits at the top of that scale. The catch is that you cannot declare a standard into existence. Standards get adopted when the standard gates something people want, and get ignored when the body issuing them has only authority.

## What Duolingo does

- **One scale, two products.** The in-app Duolingo Score runs on the same **0–160** scale as the Duolingo English Test, and every band is mapped to CEFR levels with concrete can-do statements. Company strategy names the pillar "Become the proficiency standard," with the stated ambition of people describing themselves as "a Duolingo French 70" instead of "intermediate." The DET is accepted by **5,500+ institutions including 97 of the top 100 U.S. universities** — which is what makes the in-app number mean something. Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-company-strategy (Duolingo blog, 2025-04-07; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **Documents the failure mode of decree.** Duolingo's history of French contrasts two mechanisms. The Académie française, founded **1635**, has issued approved French replacements for English borrowings for centuries — *courriel* for e-mail, *réunion* for meeting — and Duolingo calls those attempts "fabulously unsuccessful." What actually standardized French was the **1539** Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts making it the required administrative language, plus enforcing Academy grammar on exams, administrative documents and job access. Even so, in the 17th century the vast majority of people in France still could not hold a simple conversation in the "national language." Source: blog.duolingo.com/history-of-french (Duolingo blog, 2022-07-11; accessed 2026-09-22)

Tension: the decree mechanism is not zero-effect. Duolingo notes Quebec's OQLF gets "slightly more respected" compliance than the French Academy — *téléphone intelligent* over *smartphone* — attributed to aggressive 20th-century language planning. Sustained institutional pressure does move the needle, just far less than incentive alignment does. And the alignment only pays if the external credential is itself accepted, which is the slow work in [[own-the-gate]].

## The transferable pattern

1. **Peg, don't invent.** Your number should be readable by someone who has never used your product, because it shares a scale with something they already trust.
2. **Adoption follows payoff, not legitimacy.** Publishing a preferred unit changes nothing. A unit that gates a job, an admission, a contract or a listing changes every individual decision to comply.
3. **The goal is the vocabulary.** You have won when people describe themselves in your units in rooms where your product is not present.
4. **Pressure without payoff buys a little.** Repeated official endorsement moves compliance somewhat. Do not mistake that margin for the mechanism.

## Apply to your product

- What number does your product show users, and would it mean anything to someone outside the product?
- Is there an existing external scale or credential in your category you could peg to instead of inventing your own?
- What would have to become impossible without your unit before anyone adopted it?
