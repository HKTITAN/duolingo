# Distribution Through Intermediaries

## Concept

Some of your users carry a cohort behind them: the teacher with thirty students, the manager with a branch, the specialist whose recommendation forty people follow. Reaching one of them is worth reaching many ordinary users, which changes two things. First, the pitch — the intermediary is both the buyer and the gatekeeper, so a replacement pitch makes them the loser in their own purchase decision. Frame the product as instrumentation for a judgment they retain. Second, the roadmap — an intermediary optimizing across a whole cohort, under constraints you never see, will invent usage your own research would not have surfaced. Their workarounds are requirements.

## What Duolingo does

- **Positions as augmentation, explicitly.** Duolingo for Schools is free for teachers and students and framed as augmenting any curriculum: "Duolingo believes teachers know their students best." It provides assignment creation and visibility into student activity to inform the teacher's own instruction, rather than prescribing a sequence. Free since **2015**; the 2021 release was built on **six years of teacher feedback**. Source: blog.duolingo.com/introducing-duolingo-for-schools-the-ultimate-partner-for-language-teachers (Duolingo blog, 2021-05-25; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **Studies what intermediaries invent and publishes it back.** From its teacher network, including a large educators group on Facebook, Duolingo documented **3 invented patterns**: repurposing the product's odd sentences offline for storytelling and fill-in-the-blank games; a certificate-and-prize system that drives study outside class; and a weekly book-club format reviewing one story together, followed by guided discussion. Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-teachers-use-duolingo-in-class (Duolingo blog, 2022-09-15; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **Inside organizations, adoption runs on visibility, not mandate.** Space Center Houston runs mixers where crew exchange usernames, monthly paired XP challenges, "streak weeks" and small prizes. Continental Floral Greens — a workforce roughly **80% Spanish-speaking** — runs inter-branch challenges, made a bilingual company cookbook, and its CEO maintains a **300+ day streak** to model it from the top. A City of Ottawa employee is past a **700+ day streak**; the city has offered it since **2022**. Other deployments span **bartaco's 30+ locations** and **Giant Eagle's 470+ stores**. Source: blog.duolingo.com/businesses-use-duolingo (Duolingo blog, 2025-11-26; accessed 2026-09-22)

Tension: visibility-driven adoption is voluntary, so it reaches the enthusiastic and systematically misses the people with the most to gain who would never opt in. A mandate would reach them, and produces compliance that stops the day enforcement lapses. There is no version of this that gets both.

## The transferable pattern

1. **Count who is behind each user.** A user who serves forty people is a different acquisition unit from a user who serves themselves, and deserves a different product surface.
2. **Never pitch replacement to the gatekeeper.** Give them data about the people they are responsible for and let them decide what to do with it. The barrier is their standing, not your features.
3. **Their workarounds are your roadmap.** Go find what they built out of your product with tape, and ship it properly. It encodes constraints your research cannot see.
4. **Inside an organization, make progress visible instead of mandating time.** Each visible participant advertises the tool laterally, so adoption compounds rather than needing repeated top-down pushes — and accept that the voluntary version leaves a gap.

## Apply to your product

- Which of your users have other people depending on them, and do you know how many?
- Does your pitch to those users read as instrumentation for their judgment, or as a substitute for it?
- What have your power users built on top of your product that you have never shipped as a feature?
