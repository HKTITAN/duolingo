# Marketing Stunts

## Concept

A marketing stunt is a single, oversized, unrepeatable event that produces a press cycle. The economics are inverted vs. continuous performance marketing: most stunts produce nothing, but the ones that work produce more reach in a week than a year of paid ads. The skill is choosing the stunts that match the brand, executing them with the right scale, and then *letting them be rare*.

## What Duolingo does

- **Duo's "death"** (2023) — a multi-week stunt with mock obituaries, mourning users, and a comeback that produced national press coverage.
- **Super Bowl ads** — irreverent, on-brand, far cheaper-per-impression than the price tag suggests because the brand is already known going in.
- **Halloween / yearly cycles** — character costumes, wraps, and seasonal stunts that reward attention without creating expectation of always-on novelty.
- **Tied to the product, not separate from it** — the stunt always loops back to in-app activity, not just press.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **Big and rare > small and constant.** Twelve stunts a year produce ad fatigue. Two stunts a year produce news cycles.
2. **Stunts ride brand, not the other way around.** A stunt without an established brand is a confused press release. The brand has to support the joke.
3. **Plan the loop back to product.** A stunt that produces press but no in-app spike is half-baked. The bridge from "saw the news" to "opened the app" is the actual mechanism.

Anti-patterns:
- Stunts as substitution for the unsexy daily growth work. The stunt is the icing.
- Stunts that don't match the brand. Audiences notice when a brand pivots tone for a campaign.

## Apply to your product

- Has your brand done one stunt in the last year that *required* your brand specifically — that another company couldn't have done?
- If yes, did it loop back to product activity, or did it just produce press?
- If you planned one stunt next year, what would it be? Why your brand?
