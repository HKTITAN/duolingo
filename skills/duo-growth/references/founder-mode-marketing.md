# Founder-Mode Marketing

## Concept

Most large consumer companies outsource brand voice to agencies as they scale. Duolingo did the opposite: the brand voice and the social presence stayed close to the founder and a small in-house team, even as the company crossed 100M users. The result is a brand that doesn't feel committee-built.

This is one version of "founder mode" applied specifically to marketing.

## What Duolingo does

- The CEO (Luis von Ahn) is publicly visible and on-brand — interviews, social, public communication all consistent with the company voice.
- Brand and product report close to the top, not as separate fiefdoms with their own goals.
- Hiring for brand-bearing roles (writers, social leads, mascot performers) is high-bar and slow ([[../duo-culture/references/hire-slow-fire-fast]]) — a single bad hire can degrade the asset.
- Big brand decisions (the death stunt, Super Bowl creative, mascot evolution) involve leadership directly.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **Voice is too important to outsource.** Agencies optimize for client approval, not audience love. The two are different.
2. **The founder's taste is the brand's ceiling.** If the founder doesn't care about voice, the brand will trend toward whoever does.
3. **Brand and product report up to the same person.** When marketing reports to a CMO who reports to a CRO, the brand serves quarterly revenue. When brand reports to the CEO, it serves the long view.

Tension: this only works if the founder has taste and time. Many founders have one without the other. The realistic version is to hire a brand leader with that taste and protect them from quarterly metrics.

## Apply to your product

- Who in your company sets the brand voice? Are they close to leadership, or three layers down?
- Does the CEO/founder participate in brand decisions, or only product/business ones?
- Has any brand-bearing role been hired in the last year on a high bar, or filled quickly to "have someone in seat"?
