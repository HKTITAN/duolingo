# Borrowed Motivation

## Concept

Hard things have a long stretch where progress is invisible, and interest in the thing itself is rarely strong enough to carry someone across it. An external attachment supplies a reason to return that is independent of how the week went, and it converts an abstract goal into a concrete, emotionally loaded one. The companion move is to attach your material to something the audience is already consuming obsessively and annotate it, rather than replacing it — you are adding a layer to attention you already have, which costs the user nothing in schedule.

## What Duolingo does

- **Anchored a hard skill to a fandom, and the cohort grew.** Duolingo profiled BTS fans learning Korean, whose stated goals were understanding lyrics and live speech without a translator. Monthly active users of the Korean-from-English course rose **29% from June 2021 to June 2022**; Korean was Duolingo's **7th most popular** language in 2021. The difficulty being crossed is real: the U.S. State Department classifies Korean as Category IV, roughly **88 weeks or 2,200 classroom hours** to professional working proficiency. One profiled learner was at a **270-day streak**. Source: blog.duolingo.com/bts-fans-learning-korean (Duolingo blog, 2022-08-16; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **Annotates the thing rather than replacing it.** The Bad Bunny post teaches **6 specific points across 6 songs** — regional consonant shifts, code-switching where an English word carries a Spanish pronunciation, a diminutive suffix, a subjunctive, a reflexive — using the variety actually in the wild rather than the textbook standard, and closes by pointing at Duolingo's own playlist. Source: blog.duolingo.com/bad-bunny-spanish (Duolingo blog, 2023-04-24; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **Ships the companion artifact so practice continues outside the product.** Every example in the *por* vs. *para* explainer is a line from a real song — **12 song-lyric examples** across the two mnemonics — and the post ships a playlist containing exactly those songs. A parallel post teaches English imperatives entirely through **4 Dua Lipa hits spanning 2017–2024**, and Duolingo maintains learner playlists for **10 languages**. Source: blog.duolingo.com/por-vs-para (Duolingo blog, 2022-11-22; accessed 2026-09-22)

Tension: the motivation is borrowed, which means it is exposed to something you do not control. Interest in the anchor can fade independently of anything happening in your product, and a cohort acquired through one fandom does not automatically transfer to the next.

## The transferable pattern

1. **Acquire on the passion, not on the merit.** People rarely sign up for the intrinsic value of a difficult skill. They sign up for a concrete thing they want to be able to do inside something they already love.
2. **Annotate existing consumption.** An example the person already meets in the wild is easier to recall than an invented one, and every future encounter re-triggers the lesson for free.
3. **Ship the artifact that lives in their existing habits.** A playlist, a feed, a bookmark — something that keeps working when your product is closed.
4. **Price the exposure.** Borrowed attention can be withdrawn. Run more than one anchor, and treat any single one as a channel, not a foundation.

## Apply to your product

- What do your users already love that your product could attach itself to, rather than competing with for time?
- Are your examples invented, or drawn from something the reader already encounters weekly?
- If the thing you are borrowing attention from lost its audience next year, which of your cohorts would go with it?
