# Referrals

## Concept

A referral program rewards a user for inviting another. Most are bad: they feel transactional, the referred experience is worse than organic, and users learn the company values *referrals* more than them. Good referrals are quiet — they reward the moment a user was going to invite someone anyway, and they make sure the *receiving* user gets a genuinely good first experience.

## What Duolingo does

- Referral mechanics are present but rarely leaned on; the engine of growth is brand and product, not paid-out invites ([[brand-as-acquisition]]).
- When invites surface, they're tied to a real moment (e.g. asking who you're learning with, friend leaderboards) rather than asked cold.
- The referred user gets the same product, not a watered-down or invitation-gated version. The first session is for the new user, not the referrer's reward.
- Rewards, when offered, tend to be in-product (gems, streak freezes) rather than cash — preserving the brand's amateur, charming surface.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **Reward in product, not in money.** A cash referral makes both parties feel transactional. An in-product reward stays inside the brand.
2. **Surface the ask at the right moment.** "Invite a friend" cold is spam. "Add your friend who's also learning Spanish" is invitation.
3. **The receiving experience is the priority.** A new user from a referral should not have a worse path than an organic signup. Often, this is reversed by accident.

Anti-patterns:
- Referral as the only growth mechanism. It compounds, but it can't substitute for product-led growth.
- Referral programs with high payouts but poor receiving UX. Drives short-term signups, long-term churn.

## Apply to your product

- Do you have a referral program? Does it feel transactional or natural?
- For a user invited via referral, is the first experience as good as for an organic user?
- Could you reward in-product instead of in-money? What would the unit be?
