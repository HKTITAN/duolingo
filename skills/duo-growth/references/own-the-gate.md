# Own the Gate, Not Only the Preparation

## Concept

Instruction is valued only insofar as it clears a gate somebody else controls. That caps your pricing power — the buyer is really paying for the gate, not for you — and it leaves your users' outcomes hostage to a third party who can raise the price, change the format, or refuse them entirely. A credential compounds in a way teaching never does: its value rises with every institution that accepts it, which is a network effect the instruction side of the business cannot generate on its own.

## What Duolingo does

- **Built the gate as a second product.** Duolingo shipped the Duolingo English Test as a high-stakes, digitally-native proficiency exam, explicitly to displace the cost and hassle of tests like the TOEFL — the CEO's stated motivation is his own experience of that barrier as a Guatemalan applicant to U.S. universities. **3,000+ institutions** accepted the DET as of 2021, and Duolingo issued **10,000+ fee waivers** to high-achieving low-income students in the prior year. Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-joins-vp-kamala-harris-call-to-action (Duolingo blog, 2021-05-27; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **Aims the free product at the gate's threshold.** The stated course goal is CEFR B2 — named as the level at which someone could get a job requiring English — and roughly **500,000 Duolingo users in the Northern Triangle** were learning English at the time, i.e. the free product feeds exactly the population the credential serves. Same source.
- **Names it as company strategy.** "Become the proficiency standard" is one of the published strategic pillars, and by 2025 the DET was accepted by **5,500+ institutions including 97 of the top 100 U.S. universities**. Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-company-strategy (Duolingo blog, 2025-04-07; accessed 2026-09-22)

Tension: the acceptance curve is the whole asset and it is slow — 3,000+ institutions in 2021 to 5,500+ in 2025 is four years of institutional relationship work with no fast feedback loop. A credential also has constraints the teaching product never had (security, validity evidence, institutional sales), so the skills do not transfer from one side of the business to the other.

## The transferable pattern

Ask what your users are actually buying. If the honest answer is "a pass from someone else," you are a supplier to that someone else, and your margin is whatever they leave you.

1. **Find the gate.** Between your user and the outcome they want, there is usually one checkpoint owned by a third party. Name it.
2. **Attacking the gate is a different company from attacking the preparation.** Different buyer, different trust bar, different sales motion. Treat it as a second product, not a feature.
3. **Acceptance is the moat, and acceptance is slow.** The free side compounds through usage; the credential side compounds through institutions saying yes. Budget for years of the second.

## Apply to your product

- What checkpoint, owned by someone else, decides whether your users get the outcome they came for?
- If you owned that checkpoint, who would have to accept it before it was worth anything — and how long would that take?
- Does your free product currently aim at the threshold of that gate, or at some proxy you invented?
