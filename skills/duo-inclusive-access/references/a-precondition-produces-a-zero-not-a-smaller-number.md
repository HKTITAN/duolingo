# A Precondition Produces a Zero, Not a Smaller Number

## Concept

Price is a continuous barrier. People trade against it, save for it, get it subsidised. A physical or logistical precondition is not continuous — it is a filter that produces a zero. If someone cannot get to the building, cannot use the hardware, or cannot free the hours, there is no discount that converts them into a user. Teams spend enormous effort on the price dial because it is legible and instrumented, while an unexamined requirement quietly deletes a whole population from the denominator before pricing is even relevant.

Two corollaries follow. Audit requirements for what they *screen out*, not only for what they measure — most gates are administrative proxies. And audit the costs *adjacent* to your price, because an affordable product sitting behind an expensive prerequisite gates out exactly the same people.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/q-a-with-diksha-dinde (Duolingo blog, 2023-10-18; accessed 2026-09-22)

- A wheelchair-using test taker in Pune, India describes choosing the at-home Duolingo English Test because local test centres are not wheelchair accessible. The cost advantage was **secondary** — the centres were simply unavailable to her. Her university accepted the result and she completed a Master's at Sussex on a Duolingo-cosponsored Chevening scholarship.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-universities-can-support-refugees (Duolingo blog, 2022-06-20; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Refugee applicants are pushed against proxies rather than capability. Refugee children are **30% less likely to complete primary education and 50% less likely to complete secondary**, and **fewer than 1 in 20** refugee students reaches higher education. The advice to universities is to be flexible about missing transcripts and test-centre access, to use the academic support offices, writing centres and international student offices they already run rather than assume unpreparedness, and to be fully transparent about total cost — visas, flights, computers, winter clothing.
- Tension, stated by the advisor: this is flexibility on evidence, not abandonment of it. Some information is still required to make a good decision.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/det-year-in-review-2022 (Duolingo blog, 2022-12-14; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Traditional preparation is costly and prohibitive, so Duolingo built a **free** readiness library and distributed it on YouTube rather than selling a prep product.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-schools-equitable-learning (Duolingo blog, 2022-01-24; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo for Schools launched in **2016**, free to teachers forever, and ships the same platform to everyone regardless of socioeconomic status — explicitly contrasted with public schools receiving different materials by neighbourhood wealth. Built mobile-first because students increasingly do schoolwork on phones.

## The transferable pattern

Go through your requirements and sort each one into *continuous* or *filter*. For every filter, the question is not "how many complain about it" — the excluded never show up in your funnel to complain. Ask what population it deletes and whether the thing it guards against is something your existing support machinery already handles. Gates are usually chosen for administrative convenience and end up screening on documentation rather than on capability.

Then price the whole path, not your line item. Someone faces the sum of every cost to reach the outcome; the prerequisite is usually far cheaper for you to supply than the product itself, so give it away. And be wary of tiering quality by budget — it hands the weakest version to the population with the greatest need, and it adds an entire axis of gating, support and QA you then maintain forever.

## Apply to your product

- Name one requirement in your signup, onboarding or core flow that someone could be *structurally unable* to satisfy. What is your evidence that it measures anything?
- What does it cost a user to get ready to use you — tooling, training, hardware, a quiet room? Is your headline price the smaller half of that number?
- If you have tiers, does the cheap tier ship a worse outcome or just fewer conveniences? Which of those two are you comfortable defending out loud?
