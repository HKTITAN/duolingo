# A Winning Message Is Valid Only Where It Won

## Concept

A message's effect depends on local priors — about your product, about being messaged at all, about how directness reads. None of those travel with the translation, so the result of a copy test is a fact about one market, not a fact about the message. The same applies to the assumptions under a tactic: who your users are by age, and where they even live relative to the thing you sell. Treat a win as scoped to the market it won in until a re-test says otherwise.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/copy-testing-experiments (Duolingo blog, 2022-01-14; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo rewrote the German push-permission prompt to say notifications are proven to foster learning success, where the original had merely said the app needs to send them. Opt-in rose **8%**.
- Excited by that, they ran the identical framing in Spanish. **No significant impact.** They label it explicitly as not a one-size-fits-all solution.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/dear-duolingo-how-does-language-learning-differ-between-generations (Duolingo blog, 2022-05-03; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The cohort shape differs sharply by market. **India is 75% under 30; Germany is 46% under 30, with 36% aged 40 and up** and learners spread evenly across ages. Brazil, Japan, Mexico and the U.S. sit around **60% under 30**.
- The dominant band differs too — **18-22 in Japan, 18-29 in India, 13-17 in the U.S.** A tactic tuned to teenagers is aimed at a minority of the German base.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/irish-language-history (Duolingo blog, 2022-03-14; accessed 2026-09-22)

- For an identity product, demand concentrates where the thing is absent. **Over 1.9 million people across Ireland and Northern Ireland know some Irish, but only about 4% use it daily** (2016 Republic of Ireland census), and most learned it at school rather than at home.
- Interest clusters in the diaspora — Boston, New York, Chicago, Newfoundland, Argentina — plus a large number of learners in India, and Foclach, the Irish-language word game, is played in **70+ countries** including Togo.

## The transferable pattern

- **Scope every messaging win to its market.** Record the locale next to the lift. Rolling a translated winner out globally without re-testing exports an assumption, not a result.
- **Expect nulls, and publish them.** The Spanish re-run returning nothing is the useful half of the story — it is what tells you the mechanism was local. A team that only ships the positive result learns the wrong lesson twice.
- **Re-check the cohort before you re-use the tactic.** Tactics encode assumptions about reference points, device habits, tolerance for competition, and why anyone opens the product. Pull the age and behaviour mix per market first; a flat distribution kills tactics that need a dominant band.
- **Map demand by absence, not by nativeness.** Where the thing you sell is ambient, there is no felt gap. Populations separated from it carry the motivation, so your addressable audience may sit entirely outside the market you named the product after.

## Apply to your product

- Which of your currently shipped copy defaults won a test in exactly one market, and when was it last re-run anywhere else?
- What does your age or segment mix look like in your second and third largest markets, versus at home — and which live tactic assumes the home shape?
- If your product is about identity, heritage or belonging, who is separated from that thing, and are they in your targeting at all?
