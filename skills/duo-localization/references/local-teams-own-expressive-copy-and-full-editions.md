# Local Teams Own Expressive Copy and Full Editions

## Concept

Functional copy survives translation; expressive copy does not. A name, a badge, a tagline or a headline works through connotation, rhythm and wordplay, so a faithful rendering of a clever source phrase is usually a flat foreign phrase. The same is true one level up, for anything story-shaped — what counts as a hero or a satisfying arc is a local convention, and a correct translation of a story built on the wrong values lands as nothing. Both cases point at the same organisational move: the local team needs authority to diverge from the source text, not just permission to render it. The price is that your brand deliberately says slightly different things in different markets.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes (Duolingo blog, 2022-12-08; accessed 2026-09-22)

- For Year in Review, localization generates multiple creative adaptations of each learner-style name per language, then works with regional marketing managers to pick what resonates locally — **even when the winner diverges from the English version**.
- The review surface is everything the campaign touches, per locale: every screen, share card, email and push notification.
- It ships in all **25 user-interface languages**, and engineers implement per-language layout changes so it looks as good in Bengali as in English. A name that must be clever, match an illustration and fit a fixed box is a design problem solved per locale, not a string solved once.
- Stated cost: this is the single largest project the localization team takes on each year, and divergence from the source means the brand says slightly different things in different markets by design.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/2024-duolingo-language-report (Duolingo blog, 2024-12-02; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The flagship annual report ships as complete in-language editions rather than an English release plus a translated summary — the Chinese edition reproduces every section, chart caption and image description in full.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/storytelling-across-cultures (Duolingo blog, 2023-10-16; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo's learning team catalogued how narrative convention varies by culture — Japanese narratives centred on unity, interdependence and duty over personal desire; U.S. narratives on a single protagonist and self-reliance; British heroes defined by wit over physicality; Latin American magical realism; Scandinavian crime fiction built around social justice and gender equality.
- The implication for content is structural, not lexical, and it is invisible to any review that checks only accuracy.

## The transferable pattern

- **Split your copy into functional and expressive, and route them differently.** Functional copy goes through normal translation. Expressive copy — names, badges, taglines, campaign lines — goes to a local owner with a brief about intent, not a source string to match.
- **Give that owner the right to diverge, in writing.** Without explicit authority, a regional team defaults to a faithful rendering because that is the defensible choice in a review, and you get correct, flat output everywhere.
- **Re-author story-shaped content per market rather than translating it.** If the payoff depends on shared values about what a satisfying resolution is, the arc has to be rebuilt locally; translating it produces correct words and no effect.
- **Publish flagship material complete in-language.** A summary tells the subjects of your research that they are data, not audience, and local press will not cover a foreign-language document at the depth they will cover their own.
- **Budget for it honestly.** This is the expensive path — the largest annual project, with per-locale layout work behind it — and its cost is paid in brand uniformity as well as hours. If the campaign does not matter enough to fund that, ship it in fewer markets rather than badly in all of them.

## Apply to your product

- Which of your strings are doing expressive work — carrying a joke, a brand voice, a promise — and are they currently in the same pipeline as your error messages?
- Does anyone outside your home market have written authority to reject the source phrasing and substitute their own, or only to translate it?
- What is your single most important public document, and does it exist in full in the language of your second-largest market?
