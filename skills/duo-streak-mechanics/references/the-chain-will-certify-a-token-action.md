# The Chain Will Certify a Token Action

## Concept

A continuity counter measures continuity. It does not measure whether anything happened. Once the cheapest qualifying action preserves the chain, some share of your users will do exactly that action and nothing more — not from bad faith, but because the visible commitment device is what they are optimising, and it is telling them they succeeded.

The consequence is a chart that keeps climbing while outcomes flatten. The mechanic has not broken; it is doing its job, which was never the outcome. The honest response is to instrument the two separately and to say out loud that clearing the floor is not the same as progress.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/why-are-conversations-in-other-languages-so-hard (Duolingo blog, 2024-09-03; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo's own learning expert tells learners directly that real study means **"more than one review lesson with sound off at a concert / wedding / club just to keep the flame lit"** — a public acknowledgement that the company's most famous retention mechanic can be satisfied in a way that produces no learning.
- The post gives a hard benchmark for what actually works instead — roughly a college course load, **1+ hour, 3 to 5 times a week** — and the author's own pre-trip regimen of about **2 units per week over 4 weeks**, plus note-taking and speaking practice outside the app.
- Note the contradiction the company lives with. One post publishes **under-2-minute** tactics for keeping the streak on a busy day; this one says that doing only that is not study. Both are true, and Duolingo prints both rather than pretending the floor is sufficient.

## The transferable pattern

- Assume the cheapest qualifying action becomes the modal action for a meaningful segment. Design the floor knowing this, and never report the counter as evidence of value delivered.
- Keep **two metrics side by side** — one for continuity and one for the outcome you actually sell. If continuity rises while the outcome is flat, that is the signal, and a single blended number will hide it.
- Tell the user the truth in your own copy. A user who believes the floor equals progress will plateau, blame the product when results do not arrive, and leave with a story about how it does not work.
- Give the honest segment a visible next rung. Publishing a concrete benchmark for what real progress costs turns "I did the minimum" from a ceiling into a starting point.
- Watch for the scoreboard becoming the product. When the counter is the thing people talk about, defend the outcome metric in review meetings, because nobody else will.

## Apply to your product

- What is the laziest possible way to satisfy your habit metric, and what share of your active users are doing exactly that?
- Do you track continuity and outcome as two separate numbers that leadership sees together?
- Where in your product do you tell users, in plain words, what the minimum does and does not buy them?
