# Trivial Floor Protects the Chain, Not the Outcome

## Concept

Most chain breaks are not caused by lost interest. They are caused by a mismatch between what a session costs and the energy available on a particular day. So the floor — the cheapest action that still counts — has to be set against the worst realistic day, not the average one. Under two minutes is a reasonable target.

The floor's job is to keep the chain, the routine and the identity intact through disruption. It is not progress, and the product has to say so, because a user who believes the floor equals progress will plateau and leave when the results do not arrive.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/tips-for-maintaining-streak (Duolingo blog, 2022-12-19; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo publishes the busy-day tactics itself: a quick review of an already-completed node, the first checkpoint of a Side Quest, or one round of Match Madness — **under 2 minutes**. A standard lesson averages about **5 minutes**, and a **3-minute** lesson is enough to keep the streak alive.
- It states the tradeoff as an operating philosophy — low-effort options such as a quick Ramp Up Challenge or simply extending the streak **do not positively affect Time Spent Learning Well**, and are kept anyway, because learners "can't learn if they churn" (blog.duolingo.com/time-spent-learning-well (Duolingo blog, 2024-06-13; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- **One counter, many lines.** Any Duolingo lesson counts toward the streak regardless of subject, so language, math, music and chess all feed the same chain and a learner can switch without paying a habit cost (blog.duolingo.com/add-new-course (Duolingo blog, 2026-06-09; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- **A cheaper alternate format.** Duolingo added four timed math puzzles — Cash Dash, Secret Equation, Magic Squares, Math Paths — in their own tab as bite-sized practice, with **Math Paths a 1-minute challenge**, alongside Life Skills exercises (blog.duolingo.com/math-puzzles-games (Duolingo blog, 2024-09-24; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- **The gap in that launch.** Puzzles and Life Skills shipped **iOS-only** while the math course itself was on both platforms — so the habit-preservation format was missing for exactly the users who might most need it that day.

## The transferable pattern

- Specify a **minimum viable day** in seconds, not in intent. If you cannot name the action and its duration, you do not have a floor.
- Offer a second, cheaper format beside the main loop for days with no appetite for the real thing. Its job is preservation, not depth.
- **Let every product line feed one counter.** Split the chain across lines and you fragment the behaviour you spent years building; a shared counter means trying the new thing costs no continuity, so an expansion recruits from your existing base instead of competing with it.
- Accept in advance that the floor will not move your quality metric, and defend it on churn economics instead — a departed user produces zero forever, so a shallow day that prevents a break beats a deep day that causes burnout.
- Ship the cheap path everywhere at once. A fallback that exists on one platform is not a fallback.

## Apply to your product

- What is the cheapest action a distracted user can complete in under two minutes that still counts, and does it exist today?
- Which of your quality metrics will this floor fail to move, and can you defend keeping it anyway to your own leadership?
- If you have more than one product line, do they feed one counter or several — and what does a user lose by trying the newer one?
