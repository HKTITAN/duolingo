# Publish the exclusions with the findings

## Concept

A claim a company makes about its own users is attacked on methods first. The reader cannot audit your pipeline, so the only credibility signal available is whether you volunteered your limitations. Stating the exact window, the aggregation unit, the minimum-sample floor and which fields are self-reported is what converts a press release into a source. The strategic version of the point: pre-stating your biggest line of attack keeps the conversation on the results. If the limitation surfaces later, from somebody else, the story becomes the omission, and a number in circulation cannot be retracted.

## What Duolingo does

- Every edition of the Language Report carries an "About the data" note stating the exact window, that data is aggregated by country or language for privacy, which external list defines a country, that **age and motivation are self-reported**, and that **learners under 13 are excluded from all analyses** — alongside a **5,000-learner minimum per country** for inclusion in rankings.
  Source: blog.duolingo.com/2024-duolingo-language-report (Duolingo blog, 2024-12-02; accessed 2026-09-22)
- The 2022 note is specific to the digit: window **1 October 2021 to 30 September 2022**, aggregation by country or language, country definitions tied to an external list, self-reported age and motivation, under-13s excluded, sub-5,000-learner countries excluded from rankings.
  Source: blog.duolingo.com/2022-duolingo-language-report (Duolingo blog, 2022-12-06; accessed 2026-09-22)
- The disclosure does not soften the findings — the 2023 edition published its window (**1 October 2022 to 30 September 2023**) beside hard segment numbers: English #1 in **122 countries (up 3)**; Korean to **#6, +75% year over year in India**; **37%** of new Ukrainian learners citing connection with people versus **17%** for English and **18%** for French; **86%** of Japanese and **76%** of Chinese learners under 30; education cited by **40%** in India, **50%** in Vietnam, **54%** in China.
  Source: blog.duolingo.com/2023-duolingo-language-report (Duolingo blog, 2023-12-04; accessed 2026-09-22)
- The hardest disclosure class is your own supply bias. The 2016 country report states outright that it cannot separate the neighbouring-country effect from a general preference for the largest options; a footnote records that for nearly every country where English was not top-two, **no English course existed from that source language at all**, so English might have ranked had it been offered; another notes **French was available from two more source languages than Spanish** and flags that as an unfair advantage; a third credits a named user (annika_a) for correcting an earlier claim. **Three published caveats and corrections** on one post, plus a dated banner pointing readers to the current edition.
  Source: blog.duolingo.com/which-countries-study-which-languages-and-what-can-we-learn-from-it (Duolingo blog, 2016-05-05; accessed 2026-09-22)
- Tension: a floor you disclose is still a floor you imposed. Excluding every country under 5,000 learners removes exactly the small segments where the largest percentage swings live, so the published rankings are systematically biased toward markets where you already have scale.

## The transferable pattern

Ship a methods block in the same document, above the fold of the appendix, not in a PDF nobody opens.

1. **Name five things:** the exact date window, the aggregation unit, the minimum cell size, which fields are self-reported or inferred, and who was excluded entirely.
2. **Disclose your supply bias by name.** Users can only choose what you ship, so an option you do not offer reads in your data as zero demand. If you are publishing a demand ranking, list what is missing from the menu.
3. **List the confound you cannot separate.** One sentence saying "we cannot distinguish A from B here" costs nothing and inoculates the finding.
4. **Correct in public, with attribution.** Crediting the outsider who caught an error is the cheapest credibility purchase available, and it makes the next correction arrive as a message rather than as a takedown.
5. **Date-stamp and cross-link old editions.** An undated old analysis quietly becomes a false current claim.

## Apply to your product

- Write the five-line methods block for your next data release. Which line were you hoping not to write, and what happens when a reader finds it instead?
- Which of your metrics is really a measurement of your own catalogue, pricing or availability rather than of user demand?
- What would you have to disclose for an academic or a journalist to be willing to cite your number without hedging it?
