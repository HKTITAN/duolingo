# Report movement, not levels

## Concept

The top of any ranking you publish is the thing your reader already believes. Restating it transfers no information and earns no coverage. What nobody else can state is what *changed* — a rank swap, a crossover, a segment that climbed three positions — because that requires the same measurement taken twice, and only the party holding the panel has both readings. A level is a description; a delta is a claim about direction, and a direction implies a future. Lead with the delta and keep the level as context, not the other way round.

## What Duolingo does

- The 2020 global edition leads not on "English is #1" but on **Spanish overtaking French as the world's second most-studied language**, benchmarked explicitly against Duolingo's own 2016 analysis: in **2016 French was top in 35 countries and Spanish in 32; by 2020 Spanish was top in 34 and French in 23**. Second-place counts were **French 71, Spanish 70** — close enough that the post forecasts the next crossover instead of declaring one, and also forecasts Chinese overtaking Russian for #9. **Korean rose from #9 to #7 in a single year.**
  Source: blog.duolingo.com/global-language-report-2020 (Duolingo blog, 2020-12-15; accessed 2026-09-22)
- Later editions keep the framing: every finding is phrased as movement. The count of distinct languages holding a #1 country spot grew from **7 (2016) to 8 (2020) to 10 (2021)**; the 2021 edition drew on **500M+ learners across 100+ courses in 40 languages**; by 2024 English was **#1 in 135 countries, up more than 10% year over year, rising from second to first in seven countries**.
  Source: blog.duolingo.com/2024-duolingo-language-report (Duolingo blog, 2024-12-02; accessed 2026-09-22)
- The recurring US map post is built so the year-over-year change *is* the narrative. Spanish was #1 in all 50 states and DC in **2017, 2020 and 2021 alike** — a stable, uninformative level — while the actual story is the #2 slot moving: English #2 in **11 states (2017), then 7 states plus DC (2020), then 16 states and territories (2021)**, against French #2 in 35 states.
- Tension, stated in the post itself: a delta can be an artifact of your own operations. The 2020 English dip was a side effect of pandemic-driven Spanish and French growth rather than falling interest in English, and new entries on the map often reflect a newly launched course rather than new demand — there were **22 English courses** offered at the time. A rank change caused by your own catalogue is not a fact about the world.
  Source: blog.duolingo.com/whats-the-most-popular-language-in-the-united-states (Duolingo blog, 2022-02-08; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

Write the report as a diff against the last edition, and put the levels in the appendix.

1. **Lead on the crossover.** "A overtook B" is a headline; "A is largest" is a caption. Search the series for every pair that swapped, and rank the swaps by how surprising each one is.
2. **Benchmark against your own prior edition by name and date.** Citing your 2016 number inside your 2020 report is what proves the series exists, and it makes a competitor's one-off release look thin.
3. **Publish the near-misses as forecasts.** Two segments separated by one point is a prediction you can be graded on next edition, and being graded is what brings the reader back.
4. **Separate real movement from supply movement.** Before publishing a delta, check whether you launched, retired, repriced or re-promoted anything in that segment during the window. If you did, say so in the same sentence as the number.
5. **Accept that a stable top is fine.** A level that never moves across six editions is itself a finding, as long as the word count goes to what moved beneath it.

## Apply to your product

- Take your last two reporting periods: which two segments swapped positions, and did anyone inside the company notice?
- Which of your deltas would survive the question "did you change the product in that segment during the window"?
- What pair in your current data is close enough that you could publish a dated prediction about which overtakes the other next period?
