# Timely Data Publishing

The one-shot play. Something outside your product moved your numbers, or a format is circulating that your team knows the real answer to, or you are about to commission a survey — and you have days, not quarters. This skill is about shipping that single finding fast enough to be picked up, and building in the baselines, hedges and caveats that stop a sceptical reader from waving it away.

For the *recurring* index — fixed annual window, published methodology, privacy floors, the thing your category cites every year — see [[../duo-proprietary-data-reports/SKILL]]. For research that establishes your product actually works, see [[../duo-measurement-validity/SKILL]].

## Making a spike survive scrutiny

- [[references/baseline-and-hedge-the-spike]] — the prior-year window and an explicit "we're not sure" are what turn an unfalsifiable bump into a claim a journalist can repeat.
- [[references/say-what-your-score-does-not-mean]] — any number attached to named people gets read as a verdict on the underlying quality; foreclose that in the piece, not in a follow-up.

## Getting it read

- [[references/civic-cta-not-conversion-cta]] — on a serious moment, route the ask to a third party; a signup CTA is what flips earned coverage negative.
- [[references/attach-to-a-format-already-circulating]] — distribution is the expensive half, and a meme has already paid for it; supply the payload its audience wants next.
- [[references/partner-for-the-other-half-of-the-audience]] — one global number gets one story, eight per-market cuts get eight, and a co-author doubles the reach of each.

## Choosing what to publish

- [[references/point-your-tool-at-a-public-dataset]] — run an internal measurement tool over a public, topical corpus; a demonstration beats an assertion and costs almost nothing.
- [[references/pick-a-question-whose-honest-answer-fits-your-bet]] — the question is your only lever; pick one where the world's real answer already serves you, then report it straight.

## How to use this

Start with the node matching the situation in front of you, not the top of the list. A spike lands you in [[references/baseline-and-hedge-the-spike]]; a commissioned study starts at [[references/pick-a-question-whose-honest-answer-fits-your-bet]] before anything is fielded. Every node ends with questions about your own product — answer those rather than reading straight through.
