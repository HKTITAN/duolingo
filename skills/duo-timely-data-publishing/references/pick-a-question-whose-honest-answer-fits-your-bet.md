# Pick a Question Whose Honest Answer Fits Your Bet

## Concept

There is exactly one lever you get to pull without compromising the research, and it is the choice of question. Everything downstream — the sample, the instrument, the analysis, the write-up — has to be honest or the whole exercise is worthless, because a reader who suspects the conclusion was decided first discounts the entire dataset. But which question you fund is entirely yours. Choose one where the world's real answer is already aligned with your thesis, and you get to report it straight: the strategic payoff arrives as a byproduct of accuracy rather than in spite of it. Choose one where you need a particular answer, and you have bought a press release nobody believes.

## What Duolingo does

Three variants of the same move, each with a different job:

**Fund the question your strategy already depends on.** The jargon study's central observation — that English terms get adopted as business shorthand even in countries whose official language is not English, making English a lingua franca of business — is reported as a linguistic finding, then connected to Duolingo's stated reason for investing heavily in English courses, that English learning affects economic mobility. The finding is true independent of Duolingo; it also happens to be why the product exists. Source: blog.duolingo.com/state-of-jargon-report (Duolingo blog, 2023-06-13; accessed 2026-09-22)

**Aim at the false belief that blocks adoption, not at the product.** Duolingo commissioned Talker Research to survey **2,000 Americans** on attitudes to learning by age. Respondents, including boomers, put the "best age" to learn at **26** — while **43% actually feel more successful at learning now than when they were younger**, citing patience (**42%**), personal fulfilment over necessity (**30%**) and more time (**29%**). Over **25%** are currently learning a language. Source: blog.duolingo.com/never-too-late-to-learn (Duolingo blog, 2024-11-19; accessed 2026-09-22)

**Tension, printed in the post.** The data cuts both ways and Duolingo publishes it: interest is roughly flat across generations — **33% / 41% / 46% / 40%** for Gen Z, millennials, Gen X and boomers — but people actively learning collapse from **45% of Gen Z to 8% of boomers**. Belief is therefore not the only barrier; lack of time and not knowing where to start are named too. The honest version is weaker than the marketing version and much harder to dismiss.

**Size the evidence on the excluded population, not your own users.** To argue that governments should accept at-home digital tests for visas, Duolingo partnered with Public First to poll **1,900+ students in China and India**: roughly **40% of Indian respondents** cited travel time as a deterrent, **43%** said the pandemic had no effect on their desire to study abroad and **25%** said it increased it. Alongside it, the coverage map — just over **1,000 UK-approved SELT centers worldwide**, **82 countries with none**, meaning **half a billion people** must travel internationally, and single centers serving **30M+ people** in Mexico, Brazil and Indonesia. **Ireland became the first government to accept the DET for visas in 2021.** Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-english-test-global-testing-insights (Duolingo blog, 2022-05-12; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

- **Pick the question, then let go.** The framing is your lever; the answer is not. If you would suppress a result, you have chosen the wrong question.
- **Ask about the population, not about your users.** A decision-maker who must change a rule needs cover, and your own metrics cannot provide it because you are an interested party. Evidence about the affected population reframes the ask from "buy our thing" to "these people are locked out" — an argument a committee can adopt as its own.
- **Target beliefs, not features.** Many adoption barriers are beliefs people hold about themselves rather than objections to the product. No feature argument reaches those. Population data showing that people like the reader succeed does.
- **Print the number that cuts against you.** It is the cheapest credibility available, and it is what makes the rest of the report quotable.

## Apply to your product

- What is true about the world that would have to be true for your bet to pay off — and has anyone measured it?
- Which objection to adoption is a belief about the user rather than about your product, and what population number would contradict it?
- If the study you are about to commission came back against you, would you publish it? If not, pick a different question.
