# Attach to a Format Already Circulating

## Concept

Research communication has two halves. Producing the finding is the half you control; getting it in front of people is the half you mostly do not, and it is the expensive one. A format already in circulation — a meme, a running joke, a viral clip, a template everyone is filling in — has already paid that cost. It also arrives with an audience in a very specific state: curious about the thing, and unable to get a real explanation from the thing itself. That gap is exactly where an expert payload fits. You are not hijacking the format; you are supplying the one piece of content its audience already wants next.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/french-homophones-tiktok (Duolingo blog, 2023-05-11; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Built a full historical-linguistics post around an existing viral TikTok — @mexemluv's **"Ton tonton tond ton thon"** ("your uncle mows your tuna"), **five words all pronounced [tɔ̃]**.
- The payload is the part the clip cannot give you — each word traced to a **different origin**: Proto-Indo-European *towos, child-speech reduplication of *oncle*, Latin *tondĕre*, Ancient Greek θύνω. **Four distinct etymological roots collapsing into one sound.** Spelling standardized in French in **1635** and in English in the **late 15th century**, which is why the collapse is visible only when spoken.
- Ran the identical play on the German "Schmetterling" butterfly meme, and again on a Crunchyroll partnership tied to its Japanese course. The post is not a one-off; the format is a repeatable slot.

**The cheaper cousin, and its cost.** When no format is circulating, you can manufacture a hook by framing observations you already have as a forward claim. "What Will Spanish Be Like 100 Years from Now?" makes **five predictions over a 100-year horizon** — each grounded in a present-day observable — and ships with a sibling post making predictions for English, so the format becomes a series. The post admits up front that it is **"impossible to predict how"** (blog.duolingo.com/spanish-language-predictions (Duolingo blog, 2023-10-09; accessed 2026-09-22)). That is the honest reading of the format — its value is engagement, not accuracy, and the piece says so rather than letting a reader discover it.

## The transferable pattern

- **Watch for formats where your domain is the subject and the explanation is missing.** Something is circulating; people are enjoying it; nobody in the thread actually knows why it works. That is your slot.
- **Bring the depth the format cannot carry.** The clip has 15 seconds. You have the archive, the dataset and the specialists. Do not restate the joke — annotate it.
- **Move at the speed of the format, not the speed of your publishing calendar.** These windows close in days.
- **Treat it as a repeatable slot, not a stunt.** The second and third instance cost almost nothing, because the template is already built.
- **When you manufacture your own hook, say what it is.** A forward-framed claim gets argued with, which is the point — but label the confidence honestly or the engagement converts into a credibility bill later.

## Apply to your product

- What is circulating right now where your team knows the real answer and the audience does not — and who on the team is watching for it?
- How long does it take you to get a 600-word expert post out of the door, and what would you have to remove from that process to make it two days?
- If you framed what you already observe as a claim about next year, would you be willing to print how confident you actually are?
