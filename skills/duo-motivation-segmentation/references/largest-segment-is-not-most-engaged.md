# The Largest Segment Is Not the Most Engaged One

## Concept

Motivation determines how much friction someone will absorb before quitting. Relational and identity-driven reasons — doing this for a person you love, or because it is who you are — sustain effort through a long, unrewarding stretch better than instrumental ones do. The consequence is counterintuitive and repeatable: the biggest declared reason is frequently not the one that produces the most time in product. If you rank segments by headcount and build for the top row, you are building for the population that is most likely to leave and least likely to deepen.

## What Duolingo does

Duolingo collects a primary motivation at signup (school, work, travel, family) and then compares share against time spent rather than reading share alone.

- Across English learners in Brazil, Germany, and the U.S., **school is the #1 reason for Gen Z in all three countries** — but at very different strengths: 40% in Brazil, 30% in the U.S., 27% in Germany. Travel is #1 for German Millennials, Gen X, and Boomers. Family is #1 for U.S. Boomers at 25%.
- The engagement cut inverts the ranking. **U.S. Boomers learning for family spend more time than those learning for work. Brazilian Gen Z learning for family spend the most time of any group in the analysis — while school, the largest segment everywhere, spends less.**
- The implication Duolingo draws is that share and depth are separate questions about the same field, and you have to ask both.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/english-learner-motivations (Duolingo blog, 2022-08-24; accessed 2026-09-22)

**Tension.** The engagement ranking is built on the declared label, and labels are not neutral. Research covered elsewhere on the blog found that speakers of Dundonian Scots show the behavioural signature of bilingualism yet decline the label, because the category is treated as low-status — and the post concedes the boundary is "socio-political rather than linguistic," with no clean behavioural threshold to substitute (blog.duolingo.com/languages-vs-dialects-in-your-brain (Duolingo blog, 2022-09-05; accessed 2026-09-22)). So a segment can be under-reported and under-engaged at the same time, and the two are not distinguishable from the declared field alone.

## The transferable pattern

Never rank segments by size when you are deciding what to build. Rank them on at least two axes and look at the disagreement.

- **Size tells you who to onboard well.** Depth tells you who to build for. They are different roadmaps and they can point in opposite directions.
- **Obligation-driven segments are large and shallow.** People who are here because something external requires it arrive in volume and leave when the requirement expires. Optimising for them buys headcount that expires on a schedule you do not control.
- **Relational and identity segments are small and deep.** They absorb more friction, tolerate a longer payoff curve, and are worth disproportionate investment per head.
- **Publish both columns internally.** A dashboard that shows only segment share will be read as a priority list, whether or not anyone intended that.

## Apply to your product

- For each of your declared segments, what is the ratio of share to time-in-product — and which segment has the worst ratio despite being the biggest?
- Which of your segments is here because someone else requires them to be, and what is your plan for the day that requirement ends?
- If your deepest segment is small, does anyone in your planning meeting know its name?
