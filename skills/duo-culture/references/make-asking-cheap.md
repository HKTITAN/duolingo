# Make Asking Cheap

## Concept

Someone who is stuck cannot estimate how long the problem would take a person who already knows the answer, so their private cost-benefit calculation is systematically wrong: asking always feels more expensive than one more attempt. Conscientious people therefore default to grinding, and the organization pays for hours that a two-minute answer would have closed.

The fix is structural, not motivational. Telling people to ask for help does nothing, because the barrier is not permission — it is the social signal that asking sends. Three mechanisms remove the signal: a published number, a relationship with no evaluative stake, and contact that starts before the clock does.

## What Duolingo does

- **A stated 30-minute threshold.** The best advice a returning Duolingo intern reports receiving: if you are stuck on a task for more than 30 minutes, ask for help. He describes repeatedly losing hours to problems a full-timer cleared up in minutes. Source: blog.duolingo.com/why-i-interned-with-duolingo-twice (Duolingo blog, 2022-09-16; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **Two mentors, deliberately separated.** Thrive interns get a "Thrive Host" — a tenured employee who runs the project — and a separate "Thrive Buddy," an early-career peer matched to their role who handles networking, time management and career navigation, and keeps mentoring after the program ends. Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-thrive-intern-program (Duolingo blog, 2024-11-11; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **The program is scoped so failure is safe.** Hosts spend the months beforehand designing group projects that "tackle strategic problems outside of the scope of current Duolingo teams" — real value, zero delivery coupling. Roughly 25 rising juniors per class, 10 weeks, 20+ internal courses frontloaded into the first two weeks, launched 2021, with the majority converting to full-time.
- **Belonging starts at signature, not at start date.** The internship "starts the moment you sign your offer letter": incoming interns get a Discord channel, a monthly newsletter and virtual events before the summer begins. Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-summer-internship (Duolingo blog, 2022-07-28; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **The trainee role is structurally identical to the permanent one** — same team discussions, same systems, differing mainly in fewer deadlines and smaller scope. One returning intern moved into the full-time role "without much need for onboarding." First interns in 2012; 29 across five functions by 2020. Source: blog.duolingo.com/celebrating-interns-past-and-present-an-inside-look-into-duolingos-internship-program (Duolingo blog, 2020-07-30; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

1. **Publish a number.** Thirty minutes, an hour — the value matters less than the fact that it is stated. A number removes the judgment call and, more importantly, the signal: asking at the threshold is following the norm, not admitting defeat.
2. **Separate the person who evaluates your output from the person who advises on your future.** Candour about doubt or confusion is costly with anyone who influences your assessment. One relationship with no evaluative stake is where the honest questions get asked.
3. **Use the gap between commitment and start.** That window is when people are alone with their decision and their doubts. Filling it with peer contact means the first real day is a reunion rather than an arrival.
4. **Give newcomers real problems nobody owns.** Unowned work has genuine stakes and no dependency risk, which is exactly what makes it safe to hand over.

Tensions worth naming. A published threshold is a floor that can become a ceiling — people who would have solved it at minute 35 now interrupt someone at minute 30, and the cost lands on whoever is senior enough to answer. Two mentors is also two people's time per newcomer, which is the reason most organizations quietly run one.

## Apply to your product

- If someone on your team is stuck right now, how long will they grind before asking, and what makes you think so?
- Does anyone on your team have a mentor who has no say in their review?
- What happens between a signed offer and a first day at your company — and who owns that window?
