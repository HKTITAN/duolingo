# Managers Keep Practising the Craft

## Concept

Technical judgment decays without practice. A manager who can no longer read the work has to evaluate it through proxies — confidence, velocity, who complained — and those proxies are exactly what a well-presented bad decision is optimized to satisfy. The decay is slow and invisible, which is why it has to be defended with a budget rather than an intention.

The mechanism that matters is the allocation, not the aspiration. "Coding when there's time" means never, because a manager's calendar is the first thing to absorb the week's emergencies. A set amount per cycle is what survives.

## What Duolingo does

- **Engineering managers spend a set amount of time coding every sprint**, review teammates' code, and give feedback on technical documents. The expectation extends upward to engineering directors, not just to first-line managers. Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-engineering-manager-interview (Duolingo blog, 2022-03-11; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **The stated belief is general, not engineering-specific**: managers for any role should have deep working knowledge of the projects of the people they manage.
- **The consequence shows up in staffing**: an overwhelming majority of Duolingo's engineering management is home-grown, promoted from inside.
- **The leadership curriculum separates two things most organizations conflate**: management is delegated authority — the organizational power to tell people what they must do — and leadership is influence, which has to be earned. Promoting a strong individual contributor grants the first and none of the second, so mentorship is named as the strongest structural lever available and training the next generation is a current leader's responsibility. Source: blog.duolingo.com/engineering-leadership-lessons (Duolingo blog, 2026-09-16; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

1. **Make it an allocation, not a value.** A number of hours per cycle, on the calendar, with an artifact at the end — a review, a fix, a critique. Anything vaguer is cut in week two.
2. **Prefer reviewing to building.** Reviewing teammates' work and critiquing documents keeps judgment current at a fraction of the time cost, and unlike shipping a feature it does not put the manager on the critical path of a deliverable.
3. **Extend the expectation upward.** If the rule stops at first-line managers, the level above them still evaluates work it cannot read, and that is the level that sets the bar.
4. **Treat the move into management as a skill change, not a scope increase.** The behaviours that produce individual excellence — doing the hard part yourself, to your own standard — are precisely the behaviours that stop a team developing.

Tensions worth naming. Duolingo states the flip side in the same post: because the bar is deep working knowledge, external management hires are rare and harder to onboard. A craft-fluency requirement is also a real constraint on who can manage, which narrows the pipeline in exactly the places where it may already be narrow. And a manager on the critical path of a deliverable is a bottleneck — the time has to be structured so their absence at a crunch costs nothing.

## Apply to your product

- How many hours did your managers spend inside the work last month? Would they be able to tell you the number?
- When your managers evaluate work, what are they actually reading — the artifact, or a summary of it?
- Who was your last external management hire, and how long did it take them to be able to judge the work?
