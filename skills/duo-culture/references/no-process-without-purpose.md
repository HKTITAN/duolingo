# No Process Without Purpose

## Concept

The handbook lists under [[../duo-product/references/ship-it|Ship It]]: *"We'll only introduce a new process if it helps us make faster, better decisions."* The default is **no process**. New process is opt-in, justified, and revisitable. The implicit move: most companies accumulate process by default — every minor incident produces a new ritual — and the cumulative effect is a slow company that can't tell why.

## What Duolingo does

- New processes are proposed with a justification: which decisions are worse without this process? How will we know if it's working?
- Existing processes are sunsetted when they stop justifying themselves; a process that "always existed" is not protected.
- Reviews and meetings are evaluated for clock-speed cost ([[clock-speed]]).
- The cultural language is "let's add a process for this" said reluctantly, not enthusiastically.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **Process must justify itself.** "We added a review step because of an incident" is a default response, not a thoughtful one. The question: does this review actually prevent the incident, or just produce documentation theater?
2. **Process should sunset.** Anything you added six months ago should be re-evaluated. If it's not paying its cost, cut it.
3. **Some friction is good.** Process that *forces* the decision-maker to think (a written proposal, a pre-mortem) often pays for itself. Process that just routes decisions to the same people through a heavier path doesn't.

A useful test: count the meetings on a senior person's calendar this week. How many would they keep if the choice were theirs? That's your process surplus.

## Apply to your product

- What process did your team add this quarter? What does it cost weekly? Did it improve any decision?
- Is there a process you've inherited that you'd cut if the choice were yours?
- Does your team feel like it has too much process or too little? Why?
