# The Green Machine

## Concept

The Duolingo Handbook ends with *The Green Machine* — the operating model the company runs at every level. It's a six-step loop:

1. **Staff it with great people**
2. **Define success**
3. **Set guardrails and think long-term**
4. **Build the thing and set up feedback loops**
5. **Execute with urgency and excellence**
6. **Double down on what works, stop what doesn't**

Each step maps to one of the five principles. The Green Machine is the *machine*; the principles are the parts.

## What Duolingo does

- The loop is taught explicitly to new hires; it's the language teams use to talk about how work happens.
- Every team / project / experiment runs some version of these six steps; the model is fractal.
- The "stop what doesn't" half of step 6 is treated with as much weight as the "double down" half — see [[../duo-product/references/kill-criteria-product]].
- Process additions are evaluated against the loop: does this make any of the six steps better? If not, cut.

Three rules:

1. **Run the loop, don't skip steps.** Most failed projects skipped one step (often "define success" or "stop what doesn't").
2. **The loop is fractal.** A weekly experiment runs through it. So does a yearly project.
3. **Loops compound.** Teams that complete the loop quickly run more loops; speed of learning becomes the org-level competitive advantage.

## Apply to your product

- Map your team's last project against the six steps. Which steps were strong? Which were skipped or compressed?
- Does your team have a shared language for *how work happens*, or is it implicit?
- What's the loop time on a typical experiment or project? Is the slow step in build, decision, or shipping?
