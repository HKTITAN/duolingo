# Ownership Clarity (Culture)

## Concept

The product version of this principle ([[../duo-product/references/ownership-clarity]]) is about features. The cultural version is about *everything else*: meetings, decisions, rituals, projects without obvious feature-shape, cross-functional initiatives, internal tools. The same rule — one owner, named, known — applies. Without it, internal work decays into ambient responsibility that nobody actually carries.

## What Duolingo does

- Every recurring meeting has a stated owner; ownership is on the calendar entry.
- Internal tools, rituals, and processes have a person whose name is on them.
- "Whose call is this" has a fast answer at every level of the company.
- Ownership is *transferable* — it's not a permanent identity, but it's never absent.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **No diffuse ownership.** "The team owns it" is the same as "no one owns it." Pick a name.
2. **Ownership = authority.** An owner without authority to decide is a bottleneck. The two come together or not at all.
3. **Ownership is visible.** Documenting ownership matters. If a new team member can't find out who owns the deploy process, the doc is the bug.

Anti-patterns:
- Steering committees as ownership. A committee can advise; only individuals can own.
- Ownership-by-org-chart. The person whose box has the function in it is *not necessarily* the owner.
- "Acting owner" indefinitely. Either the role is owned or it's vacant; "acting" should be measured in weeks.

## Apply to your product

- Pick three recurring rituals (meetings, reports, processes) in your team. Who owns each? Would your team agree?
- Is there a process running on autopilot with no current owner? What happens when it breaks?
- Has anyone been given ownership of a function but not the authority to decide?
