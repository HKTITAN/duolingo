# Async vs. Meetings

## Concept

A meeting is the right tool for converging — getting people aligned in real time on something that needs back-and-forth. A document is the right tool for thinking — letting the author work the idea before exposing it, letting readers consume on their own time. Most teams over-use one and under-use the other. The discipline is matching the tool to the work.

## What Duolingo does

- Reviews and decisions happen in tight, well-prepared meetings; people read materials *before*, not during.
- Substantive thinking happens in writing — proposals, RFCs, post-mortems are written, then meeting time is for resolution, not exposition.
- Meeting load is treated as a clock-speed cost ([[clock-speed]]); a calendar of back-to-back meetings is a problem to solve, not a status symbol.
- Recurring meetings are reviewed for value — "is this still earning its slot" is a normal question.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **Default to async.** A meeting is the burden of proof, not the default mode of work.
2. **No meeting without a document.** If the work doesn't justify a pre-read, the meeting probably doesn't justify itself.
3. **Meetings end with a decision.** A meeting that closes with "let's discuss again next week" was probably the wrong format. Write a doc, decide async.

Anti-pattern: "let's hop on a call" as the universal response. Often, what's needed is a comment thread or a 200-word doc.

## Apply to your product

- Of your recurring meetings, how many would survive if attendance were optional?
- Has any decision in your team been delayed because the right format was a meeting and the calendar didn't allow it? Could it have been a doc?
- Is "let's hop on a call" your team's default or your team's exception?
