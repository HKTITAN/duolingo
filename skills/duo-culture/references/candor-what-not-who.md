# Candor: What, Not Who

## Concept

The handbook's [[../duo-product/references/raise-the-bar|Raise the Bar]] lists: *"We give candid feedback that focuses on the 'what' and not the 'who.'"* The rule is small and load-bearing. Without it, candor degrades into criticism of people, which makes feedback unsafe and so feedback stops happening.

The discipline: feedback names the work, the impact, the alternative. It does not name character traits. It is the same rule whether the recipient is junior or the CEO.

## What Duolingo does

- Reviews and code/design critiques surface concrete issues — "this flow has three steps where one would do" — not character judgments.
- Disagreement is routed to evidence ([[../duo-experimentation/references/show-dont-tell]]) when possible, removing the need for personal challenge.
- Status doesn't shield from feedback; the same rule applies up and down the org chart.
- Feedback culture is taught explicitly to new managers; it doesn't transmit by osmosis.

## The transferable pattern

A three-part feedback template:

1. **What** — the specific behavior or artifact, named concretely.
2. **Impact** — what it produced (downstream cost, missed opportunity, confusion).
3. **Alternative** — what would be better. (If you can't name one, the feedback is incomplete.)

Three rules:

1. **Never personal.** "You're sloppy" is not feedback; it's a verdict. "This shipped without an empty state" is feedback.
2. **Same rules up and down.** A junior person should be able to critique a senior person's work using the same protocol. If the protocol breaks under power, it doesn't work.
3. **Feedback is a skill, not a personality trait.** Some people are naturally direct; everyone can learn to be useful. The training is real.

## Apply to your product

- Read your last performance review (given or received). Did it focus on what, or on who?
- Does feedback flow upward in your team? Could a junior person give a senior person honest feedback safely?
- Is "candor" a stated value with no protocol, or is the *how* taught?
