# Clock Speed

## Concept

The handbook lists *"We optimize 'clock speed,' minimizing gaps between steps to maintain momentum"* under [[../duo-product/references/ship-it|Ship It]]. The metric isn't *speed of any one step* — it's the *gap between steps*. Idle time. Wait time. Time projects sit while no one's actively pushing them. Most slow companies aren't slow at any one task; they're slow in the gaps.

## What Duolingo does

- Reviews are scheduled close to outputs — a finished experiment is reviewed within a week, not next quarter.
- Decision-makers are accessible — the answer to "who decides this" is always close to the team doing the work.
- Process additions face a clock-speed test: does this slow the loop? If yes and it doesn't justify the cost, it's cut ([[no-process-without-purpose]]).
- The cultural norm is to *hate* idle work — a project sitting unattended for a week is a problem, not a normal state.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **Measure the gaps, not the work.** Most teams optimize task duration. The bigger lever is reducing idle time between tasks.
2. **Make decisions visible.** Hidden decisions take longer because nobody knows they're waiting on one. Visible decisions get pushed.
3. **Clock speed is leadership's responsibility.** Engineers can ship faster, but only leadership can compress the gaps between hand-offs.

A useful exercise: take your last project, plot when each step *finished* and when the next step *started*. Sum the gaps. That's your clock-speed problem.

## Apply to your product

- For your last shipped feature, how much time was spent waiting vs. doing?
- What's your team's biggest gap-step (review? deploy? decision?)?
- Are decisions in your org visible — can a team member tell who's currently blocking what?
