# One Legible Number Plus A Content Manifest

## Concept

Multiple internal currencies — points, levels, units completed — measure activity, not attainment. They cannot be compared across tracks or against a goal, so they tell a user how busy they have been and nothing about where they stand. One number with a known ceiling and a known real-world meaning fixes that. But a number alone only supports pacing. When someone wants to revisit a specific capability, they need to navigate by subject matter, which means the level has to expose what it contains. And the third piece is the one teams skip: disclose what your grader is actually optimizing for, because a user who cannot see the grader's objective reads every mismatch as the product being broken.

## What Duolingo does

- Duolingo Score reflects the level of vocabulary and grammar a learner is working with, is comparable with friends and across courses, and tops out at **120** — described as the level needed to get a job in the language. Internal efficacy studies now recruit by Score band rather than by lessons completed, with Video Call research cohorts defined as **Score 60–71** and **Score 45–55**.
- Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-updates (Duolingo blog, 2025-04-10; accessed 2026-09-22)
- Tapping a section's Score reveals that section's CEFR level and the specific vocabulary and grammar it covers — French Section 2 is A1 content and includes negation — so a learner can jump back to a topic instead of to a position on a bar.
- Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-to-review-lessons-on-duolingo (Duolingo blog, 2024-09-10; accessed 2026-09-22)
- On grader disclosure, Duolingo published an 8-part explanation of why a general translation tool's output gets marked wrong in a lesson — differences in goals, gender, number, formality, meaning, dialect, plus user typos and software overcorrection — showing that one English sentence such as "You are tall" has many valid Spanish forms while the general tool returns only the masculine singular. A submitted translation is matched against a list of acceptable answers **sometimes numbering into the thousands**.
- Tension, admitted in the same post: word-tile exercises have room for only one answer, so Duolingo's displayed answer is also a collapse of that space — just one chosen for teaching reasons rather than statistical ones.
- Source: blog.duolingo.com/is-google-translate-wrong (Duolingo blog, 2025-08-19; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

- **Ship one headline number, with a ceiling and an external meaning.** "Where am I, out of what, and what does the top get me" has to be answerable in a glance. A ceiling turns diffuse effort into a destination; an outside meaning makes the destination worth reaching.
- **Make it comparable across tracks and across people, or do not call it a score.** If two users on different paths cannot line their numbers up, you have a progress bar with ambitions.
- **Attach a manifest to every level.** What this level contains, in the vocabulary of the domain rather than of your data model, so a user can navigate by what they need instead of by how far they got.
- **Use the same scale internally.** When your research and your reporting recruit and segment by the public number, the number gets audited by your own team continuously. Parallel internal and external scales drift, and the drift always surfaces publicly.
- **Publish what the grader optimizes for.** Any automated judge collapses a space of valid responses to a default, and the user cannot see the collapse. Either show the full accepted set or state the objective — how many alternatives you accept, which dimensions you enforce, and where your own format forces you to pick one. Naming your own collapse costs less than having it discovered.

## Apply to your product

- What is your one number, what is its ceiling, and what does hitting the ceiling let a user claim to someone who has never used your product?
- Can a user find out what a given level actually contains, or only how far through it they are?
- Where does your grading or scoring logic silently pick one of several defensible answers, and what would it cost you to publish that objective before a user finds the edge case?
