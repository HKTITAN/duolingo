# Design So Cramming Cannot Move The Score

## Concept

If a measurement rewards preparation for the measurement, it is measuring preparation. Once a score can be moved by study aimed at the score itself, three things follow: a prep industry forms around it, the number decouples from the ability it claims to represent, and whoever can afford preparation gets an inflated result. Building the score so only the underlying ability moves it removes the incentive and the inequity in one step — and then you have to tell people plainly, or they will keep buying prep anyway. The corollary is that what you own is not the scoring screen. It is the whole journey: what it costs, how long it takes, the wait, and how the result arrives.

## What Duolingo does

- Duolingo reframed "test prep" as "test readiness" for the Duolingo English Test and stated it flatly: intense studying right before the test will not change your score; your score improves as your English proficiency improves.
- The design backs the claim. The test is adaptive, has a single section, and uses short predictive exercises rather than long passages, so there is no format to drill. Around it: **$49**, **1 hour**, results within **2 days**, a free practice test with **unlimited retakes**, and unlimited free score sending to institutions.
- Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-english-test-readiness (Duolingo blog, 2021-10-14; accessed 2026-09-22)
- Duolingo coined **TTX** (test-taker experience), explicitly distinguished from UX, spanning test administration through score reporting. Naming the wider scope is what turned price, turnaround time, preparation materials and result delivery into design surfaces someone owns: free readiness resources, a lower price point, shorter testing time (**one hour**), fast score turnaround (**48 hours**), and accessibility accommodations all came out of it.
- The same team treats fairness as an experience variable, accounting for how experience varies by culture, first language and computer setup during item development and QC.
- Source: blog.duolingo.com/what-if-tests-were-delightful (Duolingo blog, 2021-09-14; accessed 2026-09-22)
- Tension worth keeping: a cram-proof score is also a score a motivated user cannot improve quickly, which is genuinely worse news for anyone facing a deadline. Duolingo absorbs that cost by making retakes cheap and fast rather than by pretending preparation helps.

## The transferable pattern

- **Test the score against the preparer.** Ask what a well-resourced, motivated person could do in two weeks to move their number without changing the thing you claim to measure. Whatever they could do is your specification defect, not their cheating.
- **Remove the drillable surface.** Predictable item order, a fixed format, a memorizable pool and a published rubric with weights are all levers for preparation rather than ability. Each one you remove shrinks the gap between the number and the thing.
- **State it publicly that preparation will not help.** Silence lets a prep market grow around your number, and that market's existence is itself evidence to outsiders that the number is gameable.
- **Scope the experience as the whole journey.** Anticipation before, and the outcome afterwards, are most of what a high-stakes user actually experiences. An interface optimized in isolation can be excellent while the product stays miserable — so price, wait time, turnaround, preparation materials and delivery of the result each need a named owner.
- **Treat turnaround as a fairness feature, not a nicety.** The wait is when people buy unnecessary preparation and make worse decisions; shortening it removes both.
- **Account for who the setup favours.** Conditions, defaults, device and cultural assumptions vary by user, and unexamined ones become a quiet tax on some groups. Audit them where items are built, not after complaints arrive.

## Apply to your product

- What could a determined user do in two weeks to raise their number without getting any better at the thing it measures — and how much of that is currently possible?
- Have you ever told users plainly that preparing for your measurement will not help, and if not, what is the third-party prep market around your product currently selling?
- Who owns the minutes before and after your core flow — the price, the wait, the delivery of the result — and could they name what that stretch feels like today?
