# Put the Number Where Engineers Already Look

## Concept

Cost is generated by thousands of small local decisions, and only the person who made each decision can cheaply reverse it. A central team has to first discover intent — why is this scaled to 40 instances, who needed this retained forever. The engineer who made the call already knows.

So the lever is not the sophistication of the analysis. It is **how many people see the number, in a place they were already looking**. Visibility converts a specialist project into ambient behaviour.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/reducing-cloud-spending (Duolingo blog, 2024-10-30; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo **bought** per-line-item cost decomposition (CloudZero) rather than building it, then **extended coverage past AWS** to services like OpenAI.
- They **folded costs into their existing metrics ecosystem** — the dashboards engineers already had open — and sent **weekly per-team reports**.
- Result: **20% annualized cloud savings within a few months**, on a base of millions of dollars a year, with **dozens of engineers contributing**. Not one five-person team finding one huge win.
- **Tension.** Their very first discovery was a **staging environment costing more than its production counterpart**, because someone scaled it up for a test and forgot. Nothing about that required sophistication. The failure was that nobody was looking.

On framing the same number for different rooms, their FinOps team expresses unit economics **per request and per instance for engineers**, and **per daily active user, per session and per feature for Finance** — the same figure in the wrong unit doesn't get funded (blog.duolingo.com/finops (Duolingo blog, 2025-09-08; accessed 2026-09-22)).

## The transferable pattern

1. **Buy attribution before building it.** Attribution is undifferentiated plumbing with a payback window measured in weeks. Build only what no vendor covers.
2. **Route the number to the existing surface.** A new dashboard nobody opens is worse than a slightly worse number in a channel people already read.
3. **Push, don't publish.** A weekly per-team digest reaches people who would never query a cost tool. Per-team so it is legible, weekly so it is not noise.
4. **Translate into the decision-maker's currency.** Cost per request for the people who write the request path, cost per active user or per transaction for the people who approve budgets. Doing the translation yourself removes the step where the case usually dies.
5. **Expect the first findings to be embarrassing.** Forgotten test environments and abandoned resources dominate the early wins. That is a sign the method is working, not that your team is careless.

## Apply to your product

- Where do your engineers already look every day, and is any cost number there?
- Name the three people who could each cut 5% of your bill this week. Do they receive any figure that would tell them which 5%?
- What is your cost per active user, and per request? If you have one but not the other, half your audience can't act on it.
