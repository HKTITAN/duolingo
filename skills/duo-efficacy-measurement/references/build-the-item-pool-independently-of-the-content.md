# Build the Item Pool Independently of the Content

## Concept

If your assessment is assembled from the same material you shipped, a user can score well purely by having seen that exact item before. That result tells you they remember your content — it tells you nothing about whether they can do anything with it. An item pool authored independently of the curriculum functions as a holdout set, and a holdout set is the only structure that can distinguish teaching from your users memorizing your own examples.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-does-duolingo-measure-learning (Duolingo blog, 2018-08-10; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The Learning Quiz draws from a pool of beginner, intermediate and advanced items covering vocabulary, grammar, reading and listening, described as **"created independently of other Duolingo content to avoid biased results."**
- It is delivered as **5 questions, surfaced every 2 weeks**, inside the product rather than as a separate research study — so the measurement runs continuously on the live population instead of once per research cycle.
- Scores are reported on a **0-1 scale** and tracked as a population average over time, which is what makes the two-week cadence useful: the unit of analysis is the trend line, not any single sitting.

The tension is stated outright in the same post: **5 questions cannot analyze one user's progress.** The instrument is valid only as a population average. That is a genuine cost — it can validate the product and can never be shown to an individual as their score, so it earns no user-facing value and must be justified entirely by what it tells the team.

## The transferable pattern

Separate the people who write your content from the people who write your test, and separate the two artifacts.

- **Contaminated items flatter you.** Any item that also appears in your material measures recall of your material. Reuse is the default failure because it is the cheapest way to get an item pool.
- **Independence is the property, not difficulty.** The pool should target the same underlying ability at the same level — it just must not be drawn from the same source. If an independent author covering the same skill produces items your users fail, you did not teach the skill, you taught the examples.
- **Run it continuously and thinly.** A small number of items surfaced on a recurring schedule inside the product gives you a moving population average, which is more useful than an occasional deep study and far cheaper to sustain.
- **Accept that the output may be team-only.** An instrument too thin to score an individual is still a valid population measure. Do not inflate it into a user-facing score to justify its existence — that is exactly how a measurement becomes a thing users optimize against.

## Apply to your product

- Where did the questions in your current success measure come from, and would anything change if someone outside your content team wrote them?
- Could you sample a handful of independent items into the product on a recurring cadence, rather than running one large study per year?
- If the resulting number can never be shown to a user, is the team still willing to pay for it — and who reads it when it moves?
