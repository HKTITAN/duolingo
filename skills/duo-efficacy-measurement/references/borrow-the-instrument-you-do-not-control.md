# Borrow the Instrument You Do Not Control

## Concept

A metric you invented is unfalsifiable, and no institution will price it. Nobody outside your company can convert your units into theirs, so a self-defined number cannot be checked by a skeptic and therefore cannot be cited by a journalist, a procurement committee, or a regulator. The fix is to run your own users through the incumbent's instrument and compare them against an already-published study of the incumbent's users. The skeptic's own yardstick produces your number.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/effective-intermediate-learning (Duolingo blog, 2021-07-31; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Learners who had just finished Unit 7 of French and Spanish took **ACTFL reading and listening tests — the same tests** U.S. university students took in the published Tschirner (2016) study.
- Sample screened hard: **n = 340** eligible learners, all with little or no prior knowledge and **no other apps, classes, or materials**. Unit 7 (all of A1, all of A2, half of B1) produced reading and listening comparable to a **fifth semester** of university study, with Duolingo learners outperforming university students in Spanish reading, Spanish listening, and French listening.
- An earlier study reported the intermediate point rather than only the endpoint: **273 learners** with no prior Spanish, Duolingo-only, assessed independently on reading and listening — end of A2 content matched roughly **4 university semesters**, half of B1 matched **5** (blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-teaches-5-semesters (Duolingo blog, 2023-01-20; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- The public claim is stated in borrowed units — "five sections is equivalent to five semesters of university language classes", plus significant improvement in reading, writing, listening and speaking after **three months** (blog.duolingo.com/how-well-does-duolingo-work (Duolingo blog, 2024-11-14; accessed 2026-09-22)).

Two tensions, both visible in the same posts. The screening that makes the result clean also makes it narrow: this is a self-selected population of people who used the product alone and reached Unit 7, not a random sample of signups. And the borrowed instrument reports where you lose — **reading scores outran listening scores at both checkpoints**, an uneven profile a single headline number would have hidden.

## The transferable pattern

- **Find the incumbent's instrument.** Whatever your category's established alternative is measured by — a certification exam, a clinical scale, an industry benchmark suite — that is the instrument. Do not build a better one.
- **Find an already-published result** for the incumbent's users on that instrument. That is your comparison arm, obtained without running a control group you cannot afford.
- **Screen the confound out.** Exclude anyone using competing tools alongside yours, then say in public that you did, and say what it costs you in generality.
- **Publish an intermediate point, not just the endpoint.** A result measured partway along the journey tells every user still mid-journey that their remaining effort pays — an efficacy result doing retention work.
- **Report the axis where you lose.** An uneven profile is the signature of a real measurement; a uniform win is the signature of a metric you chose after seeing the data.

## Apply to your product

- What instrument does your category's incumbent already get measured by, and what published number exists for its users?
- Who in your user base is clean enough to compare — no competing tool, no prior expertise — and how much of your population does that exclude?
- Which axis would this comparison show you losing on, and are you willing to publish it?
