# Check the Distribution, Not the Score

## Concept

A measure's job is to separate people. Whether it is doing that job is invisible in any individual result and obvious in the shape of the whole distribution. If nearly everyone clusters at the top, the instrument has stopped discriminating at the high end — which is exactly where the decisions it informs are made. This holds regardless of how hard the thing feels to the people taking it; subjective difficulty and statistical difficulty are different quantities.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/is-the-duolingo-english-test-hard (Duolingo blog, 2021-06-14; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The Duolingo English Test's Chief of Assessment, Alina von Davier, states the calibration check as a single observable: **"If the test wasn't hard enough, we'd see everyone scoring very highly."**
- The observed result is the healthy shape — a **wide range of scores with few top scores** — which is what licenses the claim that the test is appropriately hard rather than merely reported as hard.
- The check is framed for a public audience asking "is this test difficult", and answered with distribution shape rather than with anecdote or with average score. The shape is the evidence; the average is not.

The limitation of this check is that it is necessary, not sufficient. A wide distribution proves the instrument separates people. It does not prove it separates them along the axis you intended — an instrument that cleanly measures the wrong thing produces an equally healthy-looking curve.

## The transferable pattern

- **Plot the distribution before you interpret any score.** Mean and median hide the two failures that matter: a ceiling, where the top is compressed, and a floor, where the bottom is.
- **Ceiling first.** Compression at the top is the more expensive failure, because high-end decisions — who advances, who gets certified, who is ready — are the ones that carry consequences.
- **Difficulty perceived by the taker is not difficulty measured by the instrument.** People reliably report an assessment as hard while all clustering near the maximum. Trust the curve.
- **Re-check after every content change.** Improving what you teach shifts the distribution upward, which quietly turns a well-calibrated instrument into a ceiling-bound one. The instrument degrades precisely because the product improved.
- **A healthy spread is not a validity proof.** Pair it with an external anchor that confirms you are spreading people along the axis you meant to measure.

## Apply to your product

- Have you looked at the distribution of your key success measure recently, or only at its average?
- If most of your users clear your internal bar, is that evidence the product works or evidence the bar stopped discriminating?
- What external check would tell you the spread you are seeing is along the axis you actually care about?
