# Intensity of Demand Beats Market Size

## Concept

Addressable market size measures how many people could plausibly want the thing. It says nothing about whether anyone wants it enough to tell someone else. An audience that files requests, runs its own polls and builds its own in-jokes before you ship has already built the identity, the support structure and the word-of-mouth channel you would otherwise have to fund. That infrastructure ships with the feature. Raw size does not come with any of it.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/finnish-a-language-to-leave-you-spellbound (Duolingo blog, 2020-06-24; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo built a Finnish course because, in its own framing, it was the passion of the Finnish learner community that drew attention to the language — not its speaker count, which is small by any measure.
- The signal was self-organized and specific. Forum threads titled "Bring Finnish to Duolingo!", "Is there a realistic chance of Finnish on Duolingo?", and one that was simply "Finnish!!!!!" existed before any commitment did.
- Duolingo then confirmed the signal cheaply with a straw poll of fans on which course should enter development next. **Finnish placed third**, behind Māori (first, already in development) and Scottish Gaelic (second, launched November 2019), and **ahead of Thai, Serbian, Croatian and Latin** — several of which serve far larger populations.
- The same logic applies to depth once you are in. After **five seasons and 40 single-topic episodes**, Duolingo's English podcast for Spanish speakers devoted an entire season to **one city, in six episodes** — subway, holidays, landmarks, neighbourhoods, a food rivalry, and moving there — on the stated basis that learning works best when the learner is interested and excited, and that this audience studies specifically to travel, work or study abroad. Source: blog.duolingo.com/relatos-en-ingles-new-york-city-season (Duolingo blog, 2022-05-11; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **Tension.** Intensity is easy to over-read. A loud forum is a sample of your most engaged users, not of your market, and a straw poll of fans measures enthusiasm among people already present. Duolingo used it to rank candidates it was already willing to build, not to decide whether to build at all.

## The transferable pattern

Rank candidate bets by evidence of self-organization, not by size of the population. The strongest signals are things the audience did without you.

1. **Unprompted requests** that name the specific thing, repeatedly, over time.
2. **Self-organization** — a forum thread, a subreddit, a spreadsheet, a workaround someone maintains, a community-built integration.
3. **In-group vocabulary** — jokes, names, a shorthand. Shared language means shared identity, which is what makes a launch spread.

A community that has already built these will carry your launch. A larger population with none of them means you pay for awareness, onboarding and support yourself, at full price, for every user.

The same instinct applies after entry. When the constraint is engagement rather than coverage, going deep on one thing your audience already cares about beats adding another shallow topic. A single unit on a topic satisfies curiosity and ends the relationship; a sustained arc creates reasons to come back. Use intensity as a ranking signal among options you would be willing to build — not as permission to build something nobody would have chosen.

## Apply to your product

- For each candidate on your roadmap, what has the audience already built on its own — a thread, a workaround, a name for the problem? Rank by that, then look at size.
- What is the cheapest poll or straw vote you could run this week among people already using your product, and which decision would it actually change?
- Where are you adding a shallow new topic when going three levels deeper on an existing one would create more reasons to return?
