# Fit the Artefact to the Moment

## Concept

Every progressive experience — a course, a wizard, an onboarding sequence — trades an immediate answer for eventual competence. That trade is only available to a user who has a future to invest in. Under urgency the same design converts into an obstacle, and the user leaves for anything that is simply a page of answers. The discipline is to notice when the moment, not the quality of your product, is what makes your product the wrong shape — and then to ship something that does not look like your product at all.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/ukrainian-phrases-english-translation (Duolingo blog, 2022-04-04; accessed 2026-09-22)

- In April 2022 the response to displaced Ukrainian speakers and the volunteers helping them was **not course content**. Duolingo translators built a free online phrasebook and said so explicitly: it is "not a resource for teaching language."
- The artefact is flat and immediately usable: **300+ words and phrases**, tabs for **English, German, Polish, Russian and Ukrainian** speakers, grouped by the stages of the journey — introductions, family and identity, communication, safety, directions, living together, legal, medical, pets, work.
- Two design choices target zero prerequisite knowledge: **phonetic pronunciation guides**, so someone who cannot read the script can still say the words, and **sentence-starter building blocks** ("I need…" plus a word list) so someone with no grammar can still compose.
- Content came from staff who had done the work themselves — volunteering in resettlement centres, hosting people. Generic content would never have produced a *legal* category.
- The same posture scaled through partnerships rather than product: the International Rescue Committee collaboration reached people across **14 U.S. cities** and **over 4,900 people** in Central America (blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-supports-refugees (Duolingo blog, 2022-06-20; accessed 2026-09-22)).

The honest tension: this artefact feeds none of the company's loops. It teaches nothing, retains nobody, and cannot be measured the way the core product is. Building it means accepting that the right response is unattributable.

## The transferable pattern

1. **Diagnose the time budget before choosing the format.** If the user needs an answer in the next thirty seconds, anything requiring acquisition is wrong regardless of how well it is made.
2. **Flat beats guided under urgency.** Reference tables, lists and copy-paste blocks outperform a beautiful flow when the user cannot afford a first step.
3. **Source content from people who have been in the situation.** Domain-generic material will be missing the one category that mattered.
4. **Design for zero prerequisites.** Assume no jargon, no setup, no account, no prior state.
5. **Let it be off-brand and unmeasurable.** An artefact that serves the moment honestly will not resemble your product, and insisting that it must is how teams ship the wrong thing well.

## Apply to your product

- What is the most urgent moment one of your users can be in, and what does your product ask of them at that moment?
- If you had to answer that user with a single flat page and no onboarding, what would be on it?
- Which parts of that page assume knowledge or state that an urgent first-time user will not have?
