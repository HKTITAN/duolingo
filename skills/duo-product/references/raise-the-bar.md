# Raise the Bar

## Concept

Principle #2 is a quality standard that applies to people, features, and decisions. The handbook lists four words for *what good looks like* in a shipped feature: **intuitive, delightful, useful, polished**. All four. A feature that's useful and polished but not intuitive doesn't pass; nor does one that's intuitive and polished but not delightful.

The discipline is collective. *Raise the Bar* fails if any one person on the path can ship under the bar without consequence.

## What Duolingo does

- Features are reviewed against the four-word bar at multiple stages — design, build, ship.
- Dogfooding ([[dogfooding]]) is a forcing function: leaders use the product daily and surface the gaps that polish would close.
- Hiring is bar-aligned: *"Better a hole than an a**hole"* — better to leave a position open than to lower the standard.
- Critical work has clear ownership ([[ownership-clarity]]) — diffuse responsibility produces unpolished products.

## The transferable pattern

The four-word test for any feature ready for ship:

| Word | Test |
|---|---|
| **Intuitive** | A new user can use it without explanation. ([[intuitive-by-default]]) |
| **Delightful** | It produces a small smile or a "huh, nice" the first time. |
| **Useful** | It solves a real user problem, not a perceived team need. |
| **Polished** | The seams aren't visible. Edges, errors, edge-cases all considered. |

Three rules:

1. **All four, not three.** "Mostly there" is not at the bar. Ship discipline is what makes the bar real.
2. **Polish is the check that catches the rest.** The polish review surfaces missing pieces in the other three.
3. **The bar is a culture artifact.** It only holds if leaders apply it to their own work first.

## Apply to your product

- Pick your last shipped feature. Does it pass all four words? Honestly?
- Does your team have a written quality bar, or do bar discussions devolve into preference?
- Whose work in your team is closest to the bar? Whose is furthest? What's the gap?
