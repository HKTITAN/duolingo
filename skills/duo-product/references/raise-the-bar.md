# Raise the Bar

## Concept

Principle #2 is a quality standard that applies to people, features, and decisions. The handbook lists four words for *what good looks like* in a shipped feature: **intuitive, delightful, useful, polished**. All four. A feature that's useful and polished but not intuitive doesn't pass; nor does one that's intuitive and polished but not delightful.

The discipline is collective. *Raise the Bar* fails if any one person on the path can ship under the bar without consequence.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/handbook (Duolingo blog, 2025-02-10; accessed 2026-09-22) — the public summary states the principle as *"Raise the Bar: To change how the world learns, we must do world-class work."*

- The public strategy names three of the four words as the standard the experimentation engine is pointed at: Duolingo runs **750+ A/B tests per quarter**, "refining every detail to make sure the experience is **useful, intuitive, and delightful**" (blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-company-strategy (Duolingo blog, 2025-04-07; accessed 2026-09-22)). "Polished" is the Handbook's fourth word.
- The bar is enforced as an unbounded loop, not a checklist. The VP of Product's third principle is *"If it can be better, make it better"*: nothing is ever fully done, teams take several design cycles on one problem, and *"until it is the best solution for our users—which is a very high bar—try again"* (blog.duolingo.com/product-principles (Duolingo blog, 2024-02-21; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- The bar has a naming rule behind it. CEO Luis von Ahn had the company **stop using "MVP"** for new launches, because "minimum viable" licenses shipping something not ready for learners. First launches are called **V1** and held to: if this is the only version that ever exists, are we OK with that? (blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak (Duolingo blog, 2024-09-20; accessed 2026-09-22))
- The V1 bar has a **numeric scope floor** under it — a first-version course ships at a minimum of 30 skills and 2,000+ sentences, and is never declared finished ([[scope-floor-for-v1]]).
- The comparison set is deliberately out-of-category. Product teams study **mobile games and dating apps** as best-in-class mobile experiences and import their standards for simple, intuitive, fun interfaces into a learning app — because users' expectations are set by the most polished app on their phone, not by the next vendor in your vertical (blog.duolingo.com/why-i-interned-at-duolingo-rebecca-hu-product-management-intern (Duolingo blog, 2022-08-26; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- Effort is tiered rather than uniform: audience and reversibility decide how much review an artefact gets ([[quality-tiers-by-reversibility]]).
- Dogfooding ([[dogfooding]]) is the forcing function — **70%+ of the company**, CEO included, uses the internal build daily.
- Hiring is bar-aligned: *"Better a hole than an a**hole"* — better to leave a position open than to lower the standard (handbook.duolingo.com).
- Critical work has clear ownership ([[ownership-clarity]]) — diffuse responsibility produces unpolished products.

## The transferable pattern

The four-word test for any feature ready for ship:

| Word | Test |
|---|---|
| **Intuitive** | A new user can use it without explanation. ([[intuitive-by-default]]) |
| **Delightful** | It produces a small smile or a "huh, nice" the first time. |
| **Useful** | It solves a real user problem, not a perceived team need. |
| **Polished** | The seams aren't visible. Edges, errors, edge-cases all considered. |

Three rules:

1. **All four, not three.** "Mostly there" is not at the bar. Ship discipline is what makes the bar real.
2. **Polish is the check that catches the rest.** The polish review surfaces missing pieces in the other three.
3. **The bar is a culture artifact.** It only holds if leaders apply it to their own work first.

## Apply to your product

- Pick your last shipped feature. Does it pass all four words? Honestly?
- Does your team have a written quality bar, or do bar discussions devolve into preference?
- Whose work in your team is closest to the bar? Whose is furthest? What's the gap?
