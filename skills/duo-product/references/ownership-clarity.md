# Ownership Clarity

## Concept

The handbook lists *"We make sure critical tasks have a clear owner"* under [[raise-the-bar]]. The principle has two halves: every critical task has *an owner* (not a committee), and the *who* is *clear* (everyone on the team can name them).

Without clear ownership, decisions stall, polish degrades, and accountability diffuses across enough people that no one owns the outcome.

## What Duolingo does

- Each feature, each surface, each metric has a named owner.
- Ownership is a thing you can hand off, not a permanent assignment — but the handoff is explicit, not assumed.
- Cross-functional initiatives have a single accountable owner even when the work spans teams.
- "Whose call is this?" is a question with a fast, named answer.

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **One name per critical task.** Two co-owners means no owner. Pick one.
2. **The whole team can name the owner.** If half the team thinks Alex owns it and the other half thinks Sam does, that's the same as no owner.
3. **Ownership is portable.** Owners change; the rule is that someone is named at every moment, not that the same person owns forever.

Anti-patterns:
- Ownership by org chart. The person whose box has the surface in it is *not necessarily* the owner.
- "Everyone owns it." Equivalent to no one owning it.
- Owners with no authority to decide. Authority and ownership must come together.

## Apply to your product

- Pick three critical surfaces in your product. Can you name the owner for each? Would your team agree?
- Is there a recent decision that stalled because no one owned it?
- Has any team member been given ownership of something but not the authority to decide?
