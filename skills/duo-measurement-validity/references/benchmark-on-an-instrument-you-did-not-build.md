# Benchmark on an Instrument You Did Not Build

## Concept

You control the definition of your own metric, so it will always flatter you, and over time it will drift toward whatever is cheapest to move. That is not dishonesty — it is what happens when the party being measured owns the ruler. A metric defined outside your walls is falsifiable, which is exactly why a sceptical buyer, regulator or journalist can be asked to believe it. The strongest version goes further than borrowing someone else's ruler — it funds the existence of people qualified to dispute your result.

## What Duolingo does

- Duolingo reports proficiency against third-party standardised instruments and against a competing institution's unit of time, rather than against its own XP. Learners finishing **five units** of French or Spanish scored on reading and listening as well as students who had finished **four university semesters**; **seven units** matched roughly **five semesters**. English learners at the end of A2 averaged **ACTFL Intermediate High** on reading and **Intermediate Mid** on listening (blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-works-with-learners (Duolingo blog, 2022-04-04; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- Its Efficacy Research Lab, staffed with PhDs, benchmarks against CEFR levels and independent tests rather than usage stats. Learners finishing the A2 section scored **560+ on reading against a 540 expected threshold** and **~540 on listening against 534 expected**; beginners hit **90% accuracy** on a short conversation test after **4–6 weeks** (blog.duolingo.com/results-duolingo-efficacy-studies (Duolingo blog, 2024-09-26; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- Where the direct use case was weak, it argued from citations it did not fund. The Latin course launch made its case with **three peer-reviewed footnotes** on transfer effects rather than vendor assertion (blog.duolingo.com/latin-alive-and-ready-to-be-learned (Duolingo blog, 2019-08-28; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- It also pays for the adjudicating field to exist. The Duolingo Research Grant, launched 2020, awarded **six doctoral grants in 2021 — up from the five planned, because of applicant calibre — plus one master's grant**, with an annual January call (blog.duolingo.com/2021-duolingo-research-grant (Duolingo blog, 2021-09-09; accessed 2026-09-22)). The 2022 cohort of **7** expanded eligibility into early literacy and elementary mathematics, the same period it was building a maths product (blog.duolingo.com/2022-duolingo-research-grant (Duolingo blog, 2022-09-16; accessed 2026-09-22)). A separate doctoral awards programme funded **14 students in 2021** (blog.duolingo.com/doctoral-award-winners-2021 (Duolingo blog, 2021-11-19; accessed 2026-09-22)) and **16 in 2022** across eight countries, including work critical of how high-stakes tests get used (blog.duolingo.com/det-doctoral-award-winners-2022 (Duolingo blog, 2022-12-16; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- When an internal bottleneck was genuinely an unsolved research problem, it released the data instead of hoarding it — the STAPLE shared task published a multi-target dataset across **5 language pairs** on a public archive and let outside groups compete, judged at a conference (blog.duolingo.com/using-ai-to-open-up-bottlenecks-in-course-content-creation (Duolingo blog, 2020-08-31; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- The tension: an instrument you did not build measures what its authors cared about, which is never exactly what your product is for. It costs money, it lags your roadmap, and choosing *which* external benchmark to report is itself a degree of freedom you can abuse.

## The transferable pattern

- **Separate throughput from outcome.** Completions, sessions and time-in-product prove people moved through the thing. They do not prove the thing worked. Only an outsider's instrument closes that gap.
- **Anchor to a unit your audience already trusts** — a recognised standard, an independent assessment, or the incumbent alternative's own unit of cost or time. Comparison to the incumbent is often more persuasive than an absolute score.
- **Fix the benchmark and the reporting threshold before you run it.** Picking the scale after seeing results turns an external instrument back into an internal one.
- **Subsidise the outside experts.** Small grants to graduate researchers in your domain cost a fraction of an internal research team and buy an evidence base, early sight of findings, and a hiring pipeline. Fund work that could contradict you; a field that can only agree with you cannot validate you.
- **Report the external number on the same cadence as the internal one.** A benchmark run once at launch and quoted for three years is a historical artefact, and the gap between it and reality widens in exactly the direction that flatters you.
- **If your hardest problem is unsolved and you hold the only data about it, publish the data as a benchmark.** You get a field's worth of attempts for the cost of organising the contest, and you keep the results.

## Apply to your product

- What is the outside instrument for your core claim — a standard, an audit, a certification, an independent test — and if none exists, what is the nearest trusted proxy you could report against?
- Which number do you put in your sales deck, and who defined it? If the answer is "we did", what would it take to replace it within two quarters?
- What research question would most damage you if an outsider answered it badly, and would you rather fund that work now or read it later?
