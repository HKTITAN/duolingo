# A Clean Cohort Buys Attribution and Costs Generality

## Concept

Your general population is contaminated by exactly the people who make your numbers look good. Motivated users arrive with prior knowledge and supplement you with other tools, so their outcomes credit your product with gains it did not cause. Filtering down to people with no prior exposure and no competing tools is the only way to isolate your own contribution — and it buys that attribution by sacrificing generality, because people who use one tool and nothing else are a minority. That is a fair trade only if you state the filter in the same breath as the result. The same discipline applies to funding — a vendor-funded finding gets discounted by default, and a vague disclosure invites the maximum discount.

## What Duolingo does

- For its speaking study, Duolingo recruited only learners who had just finished **Unit 5**, **did not know the language before starting Duolingo**, and **used no other study materials**. It then scored them on the **Pearson Versant test** — an external commercial assessment rating sentence mastery, vocabulary, fluency and pronunciation — rather than on anything Duolingo owns.
- **258 learners** completed it. **66% of Spanish and 53% of French learners** scored A2 or higher overall, so roughly half met or exceeded the target level (blog.duolingo.com/how-well-does-duolingo-teach-speaking-skills (Duolingo blog, 2021-06-01; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- The tension is structural and Duolingo's own framing concedes it. The filter that makes attribution clean also makes the cohort unrepresentative — learners who use a single app and nothing else are not the typical user, so the figure describes a population most readers are not in.
- On disclosure, Duolingo part-funded a Baycrest / York University study of adults aged **65–75** who had studied no language at all in the previous **10 years**, doing **30 minutes a day, 5 days a week, for 16 weeks**. Both the Duolingo group and a group using a dedicated brain-training app improved on executive function and working memory versus control; the result published in 2021. The post closes by naming the funders and enumerating the stages Duolingo was walled off from — it **did not contribute to or review the study design, data collection, analysis, or interpretation** (blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-benefits-the-brain (Duolingo blog, 2021-10-25; accessed 2026-09-22)).

- Note what that disclosure bought. The finding was second-order — a cognitive benefit Duolingo does not sell — and it landed with an audience the primary pitch never reaches, precisely because it read as science rather than as a product claim. That only works if the arm's-length statement is specific enough to check.

## The transferable pattern

Three moves, all about making a result survive the scrutiny it invites.

1. **Design the population to answer the attribution question, then disclose the design as a limit.** Write the eligibility rule as a sentence — no prior exposure, no competing tools, a fixed amount of use — and publish that sentence next to the headline. A number reported without its filter will be read as applying to everyone, and the correction will come from a critic rather than from you.
2. **Fix the exposure dose, not just the eligibility.** "Used the product" is not a condition. A fixed quantity over a fixed window makes the result reproducible and stops the finding from being carried by the heaviest users in the cohort.
3. **Enumerate the stages you did not touch.** "Independently conducted" is a phrase; "we funded it and had no input on design, collection, analysis or interpretation" is a list a reader can check. Specificity converts a disclosure from an admission into evidence of method.

The general rule underneath all three: every choice that strengthens attribution weakens generality, and every choice that widens the population weakens attribution. You cannot win both, so pick deliberately, say which one you bought, and run the other design later rather than pretending one study did both.

## Apply to your product

- If you filtered your outcome study to accounts with no prior tooling and no parallel vendor, how many would be left — and would that number still be worth reporting?
- What is the eligibility sentence for the study you would run next quarter, and are you willing to print it directly beneath the headline figure?
- For any research you pay for, can you name the specific stages you had no hand in? If you cannot, you influenced more of it than your disclosure implies.
