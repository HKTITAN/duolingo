# Charge for Friction Removal, Not Core Value

## Concept

There are two places a paywall can sit. In front of the core value — the thing the product exists to deliver — or in front of the friction that surrounds it. Putting it in front of the core value caps your reach at your conversion rate, because the free experience is also your word-of-mouth surface and your acquisition engine. Putting it in front of friction leaves the core whole for everyone while still selling something real to the people who want it, and it stops growth and revenue from competing inside every roadmap argument.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-technology-innovations (Duolingo blog, 2022-06-13; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Super Duolingo charges to remove ads and to add conveniences. Paid and free learners get **the same course, the same tips, the same instruction**. The stated goal is that money will never be a barrier to a high-quality education on Duolingo.
- The flagship app organically became the **top-grossing app in the Education category on both Google Play and the Apple App Store** while every piece of learning content stayed free.
- Two paid tiers sit on top of one free core — Super (ad removal, extra practice surfaces) and Max (AI features including Explain My Answer, Roleplay, Video Call) — across **40+ languages and 100+ courses that are all free** (blog.duolingo.com/why-i-interned-at-duolingo-rebecca-hu-product-management-intern (Duolingo blog, 2022-08-26; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- **The line is not pure.** Super does gate real functionality — Unlimited Hearts, Practice Mistakes, Mastery Quiz, Unlimited Test-outs. The commitment is drawn at *instructional content*, not at *all functionality*, and the difference matters when you quote it as a principle.
- Note the drift — the hearts economy described in these posts has since been replaced by an energy system, so the specific gated feature names age faster than the rule behind them.

## The transferable pattern

Split your product into three layers and price them separately.

1. **Core value** — the outcome the user came for. Free, complete, and never degraded to create upgrade pressure.
2. **Friction** — ads, waiting, throttles, manual steps, re-entry after a mistake. This is what a subscription removes. It is real value, it is worth money, and removing it costs the free user nothing.
3. **Amplification** — optional power features that make a good outcome faster or richer. Priced on top.

The discipline is to keep layer 1 from leaking into layers 2 and 3 over time. It leaks quietly, one quarter at a time, when a feature that was core gets reclassified as convenience because the revenue target is short. Write the boundary down and name what is on each side, because "we do not paywall the core" is unenforceable until somebody has defined the core.

## Apply to your product

- Write the one-sentence outcome your product exists to deliver. Is anything on the paid side of your current line part of that sentence?
- Which of your paid features are removing friction you created, and which are removing friction that is intrinsic to the job? The first kind is fair to sell; the second is a hostage.
- If you published your free-versus-paid boundary as a public commitment, which current paid feature would you be embarrassed to defend?
