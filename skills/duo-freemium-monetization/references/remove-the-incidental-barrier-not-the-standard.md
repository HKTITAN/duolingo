# Remove the Incidental Barrier, Not the Standard

## Concept

Legacy gatekeeping processes bundle two different requirements into one price of entry. There is the real requirement — the thing you actually intend to measure — and there are the incidental ones that accumulated around it, usually cost, travel and scheduling. The incidental requirements filter on geography and wealth, not on ability, so removing them does not change who can pass. It changes who can attempt, which produces a strictly larger and equally qualified population. The critical move is naming the distinction out loud, because "we lowered the barrier" and "we lowered the bar" sound identical to anyone predisposed to hear the second.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-uk-visit (Duolingo blog, 2022-10-24; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The Duolingo English Test is priced about **80% below other proficiency tests** and uses **AI-driven security** so it can be taken from anywhere — removing the need to **travel internationally to a test centre** or to **pay months of salary in fees**. The assessment standard is what remains; the logistics are what was cut.
- Luis von Ahn grounds the decision in his own experience **travelling from Guatemala to El Salvador** to sit an expensive proficiency test, which is why the barrier being removed is named specifically rather than described as general accessibility.
- The consequences of removing incidental barriers show up as reach in places nobody targeted. In the UK — **20M+ Duolingo users**, the **second-largest population of Ukrainian learners** anywhere, and **20 times the number of native Scottish Gaelic speakers now learning the language**.
- **Welsh was the fastest-growing UK language in 2020**, against a **Welsh government target of 1 million speakers by 2050** — a case where removing the access barrier aligned with a public policy goal the company had no hand in setting.
- Institutional reach follows the same shape — **over a third of US primary-school foreign language teachers** use Duolingo for Schools.
- **The tension.** Cheaper and remote means the credential's acceptance has to be earned separately, through security design and institutional trust, and that work never finishes. Removing the incidental barrier transfers the burden of proof onto you rather than eliminating it.

## The transferable pattern

Take any gate in your product — an approval, a qualification, a minimum, an onboarding requirement — and split it in two.

**The standard** is what you actually intend to measure. It stays exactly where it is. Touching it is a different decision with different consequences, and mixing the two decisions is how you lose the argument.

**The incidental cost** is everything a candidate must spend to be measured — money, travel, time-of-day, equipment, a colleague's availability, a scheduled window. Every one of these filters on circumstance. Ask of each in turn — does this correlate with the thing we are measuring, or with the candidate's resources?

Then say which one you removed, in those words. The people who will object are objecting to a perceived drop in standard, and the only answer that works is a specific description of what was cut and what was kept. Expect to spend real effort proving the standard held, because that burden moves onto you the moment the gate gets cheaper.

## Apply to your product

- List everything a prospective user must spend before your product can evaluate them. Which of those costs correlates with your actual criterion?
- Which of your requirements exists because of how the process was historically delivered rather than because of what it measures?
- If you cut the largest incidental cost, how would you demonstrate that the standard did not move?
