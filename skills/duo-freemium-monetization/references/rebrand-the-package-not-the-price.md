# Rebrand the Package, Not the Price

## Concept

A rename of a paid tier is a message that existing subscribers cannot verify. They see a new name on their account and a new name on their receipt, and they have no way to check what else moved. Ambiguity defaults to suspicion, and suspicion arriving just before a renewal is churn. The fix is unglamorous — change exactly one thing, the packaging, and then answer the specific questions a suspicious subscriber would ask, in their words, with short answers. A rebrand that quietly moves a feature or a price is not a rebrand; it is a price change wearing a costume, and subscribers will read it that way once someone posts the comparison.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/super-duolingo-launch (Duolingo blog, 2022-06-08; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo Plus became **Super Duolingo**. The launch post is structured as an **FAQ answering the exact questions a suspicious subscriber would ask**, rather than as a feature announcement.
- "Do I lose any of the old features of Plus?" — **Nope**. "Do I have to pay more?" — **Not at all, same price, monthly or annually**. Both answers are one word long before any elaboration.
- Continuity is confirmed on the things subscribers had already built around — the **family plan (up to 5 additional people)** and **gifting** both survive the rename, and the **2-week free trial** stays.
- The name was chosen for a reason that has nothing to do with English. Duolingo picked **"super" because it is a concept that is easy to translate into many different languages** — it already exists as a borrowed word almost everywhere, so it needs no explanation and does not fragment into a different name per locale.
- The aesthetic reference was **video games, anime and comics** rather than the default software convention of "Plus" or "Pro," which makes the tier legible as a power-up rather than as a business upgrade.
- **The tension.** Keeping every feature and the price means the rebrand buys you positioning and nothing else in the short term. If you need the rename to also fix packaging or pricing, do those as separate, separately announced changes — bundling them is what turns a rebrand into a trust event.

## The transferable pattern

Two rules, in order of importance.

**Change one variable.** Packaging, features, or price — pick one per announcement. If you must change two, announce them weeks apart so subscribers can attribute cause correctly. The announcement should be able to say "nothing else changed" and be literally true.

**Answer the suspicion, not the strategy.** Write the FAQ from the subscriber's anxieties — do I lose anything, do I pay more, does the thing I set up still work, do I have to do anything. Lead with the one-word answer. A paragraph of positioning before the answer reads as evasion.

**On naming** — a tier name appears in paywalls, receipts, support articles and app stores across every locale you operate in. A name that must be translated becomes a different brand asset in each market, and you will maintain all of them. Prefer a word that already exists as a loanword in your users' languages over one that is clever in your own.

## Apply to your product

- If you renamed your paid tier tomorrow, what are the four questions your subscribers would ask? Can you answer each in one word truthfully?
- Does your tier name survive as-is in every market you sell in, or does it become a different word in each?
- Which recent packaging change bundled a price or feature move that you did not announce separately?
