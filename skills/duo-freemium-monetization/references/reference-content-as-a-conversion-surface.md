# Reference Content as a Conversion Surface

## Concept

Reference content you already own is the cheapest conversion surface you have, and most companies waste it twice. First by treating each page as an isolated article rather than as part of a lattice, so arriving traffic bounces back to search instead of moving deeper into your surface. Second by putting one house call-to-action on every page regardless of who the page attracted — when search intent has already done the segmentation for you, for free. A reader who arrived on a technical reference has self-identified as serious about the problem, which is the highest-intent moment in your entire funnel, and it cost you nothing because the page existed anyway.

## What Duolingo does

- The demonstratives guide closes by linking **seven sibling guides** — common adjectives, -ed/-ing adjectives, comparatives and superlatives, possessives, describing nouns, adjective order, gradable versus non-gradable — so the page is a node in a lattice rather than a leaf.
- The same page **opens and closes with a paid Duolingo English Test insertion**, pitched on the specific objections that matter to that reader — **one hour, taken at home, accepted by thousands of institutions**.
- CTAs are **routed by the audience the page attracts**, not by the company's priority product. English-grammar posts carry the **paid** test banner at top and bottom because their readers are the exact people who need certification; general language posts carry the **free** "Download Duolingo" link; chess posts carry a chess-specific link. That is **3 distinct CTA types**, with **up to 2 placements per post** (blog.duolingo.com/types-of-descriptive-adjectives (Duolingo blog, 2026-09-10; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- The lattice has a visible hub-and-spoke shape. An **English personal pronouns hub** links out to subject, object, reflexive and possessive pronoun posts, each of which links **back to the hub and sideways to its siblings**; the adjectives hub does the same across **ten sub-topics**. Insertions carry tracking — **utm_campaign=blog-insertion** on the top promo, and a tracked download link repeated **mid-body and again at the close** (blog.duolingo.com/english-personal-pronouns (Duolingo blog, 2026-05-06; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- **The tension.** Placing a conversion path above the fold on a reference page trades some of the page's usefulness for conversion, and a page that reads as a sales surface loses the authority that brought the traffic. The placement only survives because the reference content underneath it is genuinely complete.

## The transferable pattern

Three moves, and they compound only when done together.

1. **Build the lattice, not the list.** One hub page defines the category and links every sub-topic; each spoke links back to the hub and sideways to its siblings. The cluster captures the long tail while concentrating authority on the head, and internal links keep arriving traffic inside your surface.
2. **Segment the call-to-action by page intent.** Group your pages by what the reader was trying to do when they found them, then assign each group the offer that matches. Two or three CTA types is usually the right number — one is lazy, ten is unmaintainable.
3. **Instrument every placement separately.** Top, mid-body and close are different offers with different audiences, since most readers never reach the end. If all three carry the same tracking parameter, you have learned nothing about where the conversion happened.

## Apply to your product

- Which of your reference or documentation pages get the most traffic, and what call-to-action do they carry? Is it the same one on all of them?
- Do your highest-traffic pages link sideways to each other, or does every one of them end in a dead stop?
- If you grouped your pages by reader intent, how many distinct offers would you actually need — and which page group is currently getting the wrong one?
