# Unpaid Supply Expires When You Monetize It

## Concept

Unpaid contribution is a real way to get breadth you could never fund — and it has an expiry date built into it. People contribute for intrinsic reward, which is compatible with a pre-revenue product and not with one earning money from their work. Separately and at roughly the same moment, you start needing delivery dates and content standards, and strict timelines with mandatory templates are exactly what you cannot impose on someone's passion project. So the fairness problem and the operational problem arrive together. The mistake is letting the arrangement drift past that point, which quietly converts goodwill into grievance. Plan the transition and pay out.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/ending-honoring-our-volunteer-contributor-program-2 (Duolingo blog, 2021-03-10; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo opened the **Incubator to volunteer teams in 2013**, producing courses that would not otherwise exist — **Navajo, Hawaiian, Welsh, Irish, Guarani, Klingon**. The breadth was the point; none of these would have survived a commercial prioritization exercise.
- In **2021 the volunteer program ended**. All course creation moved **in-house and paid**, and contributors were offered **paid part-time Course Specialist roles** rather than simply being thanked and released.
- Scale of the thing being ended — **over 1,000 volunteer contributors across a decade**, recognized with a **$4 million Duolingo Language Impact Award fund** shared among them.
- The operational reason is concrete. A course takes **at least 9 months to launch, usually longer**, which is a commitment horizon that only works with people you can hold to a schedule.
- **Duolingo states plainly that ending it was not easy**, and gives both uncomfortable reasons rather than only the flattering one — it is **no longer fair or equitable** once the company earns from the courses, and their own **standardization requirements should not be applied to volunteers**. The first reason is about the company's conduct, not the volunteers' performance.

## The transferable pattern

Name the expiry condition when you start, not when it arrives. There are two, and whichever comes first ends the arrangement.

**You begin earning from the output.** Not "you have revenue somewhere in the business" — revenue attributable to the specific contributed work. Past that line, unpaid contribution is extraction, however enthusiastically it is given.

**You need deadlines or standards.** The moment you have to say "by this date, in this format," you are describing employment. You can ask a volunteer for either the passion or the process, not both.

When you reach the line, the transition is the whole test of your integrity here. Pay retroactively and visibly, offer the contributors paid roles first, and give the real reasons in public — including the one that is unflattering to you. Contributors already know which reason is true, and a sanitized version tells them what you think of them.

## Apply to your product

- Which part of your supply is unpaid or under-paid today, and are you now earning from it?
- What would it cost to replace that supply with paid work, and is that number in any plan you currently have?
- If you had to end the arrangement next quarter, what would you owe the people in it — and could you say the real reason out loud?
