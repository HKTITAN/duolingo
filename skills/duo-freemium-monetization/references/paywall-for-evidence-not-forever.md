# Paywall for Evidence, Not Forever

## Concept

A paid cohort is small, engaged, already instrumented, and already paying for the privilege of being experimented on. That makes it the cheapest place in your product to find out whether an uncertain, expensive feature actually works. But the reason it is a good test bed is also the reason it is a bad permanent home — the cohort is small. Once a feature is proven to improve the core job, leaving it behind the paywall caps that improvement at your subscriber count, and the core job is what the entire funnel rests on. So the paywall's role is evidentiary. It buys you a verdict, and then it should get out of the way.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/explain-my-answer-now-free (Duolingo blog, 2026-01-01; accessed 2026-09-22)

- **Explain My Answer** launched as a Duolingo Max subscriber feature, giving detailed grammar and vocabulary explanations for a specific answer the learner had just given.
- After it "was a big hit with learners," Duolingo made it **free for all learners** — the decision was made on observed reception inside the paid cohort, not on a forecast written before launch.
- At the point it went free it covered **seven courses — Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Portuguese, Italian and Korean** — so the release was staged by surface rather than switched on everywhere at once.
- Duolingo's broader monetization arrangement puts this in context — AI features are the paid tier's headline, so this was not a spare part being given away (blog.duolingo.com/why-i-interned-at-duolingo-rebecca-hu-product-management-intern (Duolingo blog, 2022-08-26; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- **The tension, unresolved in the post.** Giving away a headline paid feature removes a reason to subscribe, and the announcement does not say what replaces it as the paid differentiator. Running this play means you owe yourself the next differentiator before you ship the release, not after.

## The transferable pattern

Decide the exit condition before you launch, and write it down as a number.

1. **Launch to the paid cohort with a stated hypothesis** about the core outcome — not about engagement with the feature itself, which will always look good in a paying population.
2. **Set the threshold that means "release it."** If the feature moves the core outcome by more than X for the paid cohort, it goes free on a named date.
3. **Set the threshold that means "kill it."** A validation chamber with no kill branch is just a place features go to become permanent.
4. **Name the replacement differentiator** as part of the release plan, funded and scheduled.

The failure mode is the feature that quietly becomes load-bearing for renewals while still being unproven for everyone else. Then you cannot release it and cannot evaluate it, and the paywall has stopped being evidence and started being a hostage.

## Apply to your product

- Which paid feature could you release free tomorrow, and what would you have to build to replace it as a reason to subscribe?
- For your last paid-only launch, what number would have told you it should go free? Was it ever defined?
- What is your paid cohort actually good at testing — and where would its engagement level make a result misleading?
