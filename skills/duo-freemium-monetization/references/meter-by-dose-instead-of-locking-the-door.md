# Meter by Dose Instead of Locking the Door

## Concept

A hard lock turns a premium asset into an abstraction. The user has never experienced it, so you can only sell it on description — and description is the weakest form of persuasion you have. A dose-based meter does the opposite. It hands over the genuine thing, briefly and a countable number of times, which creates a specific felt want rather than a vague curiosity. Then the meter runs out, and the offer restates itself at the exact moment the user is most receptive, without you having to write a single line of pitch copy.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/popular-songs-music-course (Duolingo blog, 2024-08-15; accessed 2026-09-22)

- When licensed Sony Music tracks came to the Music course in **August 2024**, free learners could play each song **up to 3 times, at a maximum of 30 seconds per play**.
- Super Duolingo subscribers got **unlimited plays and unlimited replays** of the same tracks.
- The catalogue was chosen to be recognizable rather than incidental — **Pharrell Williams, Meghan Trainor, P!nk, Dove Cameron** — so the dose lands on something the user already has an attachment to.
- Availability was **iOS and Android only at launch**, which is its own reminder that a metered premium asset still has a distribution surface to decide.
- **The tension.** Metering here is not purely a persuasion design. Licensed content carries per-use cost, so the meter is also a cost control, and those two motives can point in different directions — the dose that converts best is not always the dose that is cheapest. Be honest about which one is setting your number. And a dose set too small stops reading as a taste and starts reading as a tease, which produces resentment rather than desire.

## The transferable pattern

Pick the meter dimension that matches what makes your asset valuable.

- **Count** — a fixed number of uses, then stop. Best when each use is discrete and memorable.
- **Duration** — the real thing, truncated. Best when the value is in the experience rather than the completion.
- **Both** — a small number of short uses, which is the most restrictive and the most explicit.
- **Recency** — full access, refreshing on a cycle. Best when the want recurs naturally.

Three rules regardless of dimension. Give the real asset, not a watered-down version, or the taste teaches the wrong thing. Make the remaining balance visible before it runs out, so the user forms an intention rather than hitting a surprise. And put the upgrade offer at the moment of exhaustion, not on a settings page — that moment is the only time you have evidence the user wants it.

## Apply to your product

- Which of your locked features could a free user experience for real, briefly, without you losing the reason to pay for it?
- On what dimension does your asset's value actually sit — number of uses, length of use, or freshness? Meter on that one.
- Where is the countdown visible today? If the user only learns the limit by hitting it, you have built a wall with extra steps.
