# Attribution Decides Whether They Continue

## Concept

When something goes wrong, the user assigns a cause. If your product does not supply one, they supply their own, and the default is their own inadequacy — which is both demotivating and actionless.

Pairing the failure with its mechanism changes what the same event means. An explained failure becomes diagnostic, predicts where the next error will appear, and is interesting. An unexplained one is a verdict. Attribution, not difficulty, is what decides whether the user takes another attempt.

## What Duolingo does

- Its hyperpolyglot catalogues **5 recurring errors she still makes**, each framed by its cause rather than its correction, and states the payoff directly: **"I learn a lot from reflecting on my errors — there is a reason behind each of them — and becoming aware of that reason makes me excited to continue learning."** She closes: "taking the time to understand a mistake makes it easier to accept it and to appreciate all the unseen work my brain is doing." Source: blog.duolingo.com/5-mistakes-professional-language-learner-still-makes (Duolingo blog, 2024-01-10; accessed 2026-09-22)
- It also attacks the comparison that supplies the wrong attribution. Outcomes are what get published; struggle is what gets hidden — so Duolingo reframes explicitly that polyglot influencers show end results, classroom instructors see whole cohorts making identical mistakes, and self-guided learners see neither. It then quantifies the unfair baseline: by age 30 a person has roughly **10 million waking minutes**, of which about **5 million minutes** are first-language exposure — the head start against which users are silently measuring themselves. Source: blog.duolingo.com/are-some-people-better-at-learning-languages (Duolingo blog, 2023-07-25; accessed 2026-09-22)
- In a high-variance domain it refuses to prescribe at all. On raising bilingual children it states there is no single right way, that **"one parent, one language" is neither the only nor the best approach**, and that the balanced bilingual is very nearly a myth. It lists the variables that make families incomparable and tells parents not to benchmark against monolinguals or other multilinguals, since the anxiety kids pick up on makes them resist. **Tension:** it still gives one firm prescription despite the relativism — build literacy in both languages, because literacy skills transfer rather than compete. Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-to-raise-kids-bilingual (Duolingo blog, 2023-10-31; accessed 2026-09-22)
- Radiolingo host Ahmed Ali Akbar traces the whole arc from the inside: he believed **"I'm not good with other languages"**, and names the actual block — **"I thought it had to be perfect. I couldn't even form a sentence."** His reframe is that **"any work you do on language — whether it's vocabulary, grammar, accent, or even swear words — will be useful. There is no being good or bad at language."** Urdu was his first language, forgotten before age **2**, re-achieved by age **22**, with over a decade since and **4 years** of Arabic study. **He is explicit the reframe was not sufficient**: "it wasn't as easy as the language just coming back to me. I had to practice, integrate the language into my life, and learn the challenging writing system." Source: blog.duolingo.com/relearning-family-language-essay (Duolingo blog, 2022-12-13; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

- **Ship the cause with the correction.** "Wrong, here is the right answer" records a failure. "Wrong, and here is the mechanism that produced it" predicts the next three.
- **Surface process, not just results.** If users only ever see finished outcomes from others, the only available explanation for their own difficulty is a personal deficit.
- **Publish the unfair baseline in numbers** when one exists. A concrete head start is a cause the user can accept; an invisible one becomes self-blame.
- **Refuse to prescribe a single right way** where outcomes depend on uncontrolled variables. A rigid prescription in a high-variance domain produces guilt when it fails, and the guilt degrades the outcome further.
- **Frame progress as accumulation.** If every partial input compounds and nothing is wasted, there is no verdict to fail.
- **Do not oversell the reframe.** Removing the identity block restores attempts. It does not replace the work, and saying otherwise sets up the next disappointment.

## Apply to your product

- Take your three most common error messages. Do any of them say what caused the error, or only what was wrong?
- Where does your product show users other people's finished results, and is there anywhere it shows them someone else's messy middle?
- Is there a benchmark your users measure themselves against that they cannot possibly meet — and have you ever named it as unreachable out loud?
