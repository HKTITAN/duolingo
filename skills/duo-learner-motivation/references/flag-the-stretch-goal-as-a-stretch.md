# Flag The Stretch Goal As A Stretch

## Concept

Ambitious goals pull effort, and they also manufacture failures. Both effects are real, and most products get only the second one because the ambitious target is presented at the same status as the achievable ones.

Labelling it as a stretch *before* the attempt separates the two. The pull survives; the failure does not, because a wildcard that does not land was never counted as owed. The label has to be applied in advance — reclassifying a missed goal as "a stretch anyway" afterwards is transparent and buys nothing.

## What Duolingo does

- A Duolingo learning scientist preparing for a work trip to Germany with **4 weeks** of study set exactly three goals, and marked one of them: greet and be pleasant to German speakers; recognize information on menus and schedules; and one flagged **"⚠️ STRETCH GOAL ⚠️"** of having one small conversation.
- She scoped by reading unit titles and guidebooks to pick a **stopping point**, then back-planned from it: target **A1**, about **2 path circles per day** plus extras, roughly **2 units per week**, running **through Unit 7**. The plan was derived from a chosen finish line rather than from how much time she was willing to spend.
- **All three goals were met, including the stretch.**
- **She is candid about the ceiling.** "I wouldn't say I learned the entire German language," and for her own travel style she is "much more comfortable having a few months of language study under my belt." Four weeks cleared *her stated bar*, not a general one — the goal was calibrated to a trip, and the write-up refuses to generalize it.
- Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-to-learn-german-for-travel (Duolingo blog, 2022-11-21; accessed 2026-09-22)
- The cadence sits on Duolingo's broader learning-science guidance to set small realistic goals **every few weeks** rather than imagining a year out, and to spread practice over a longer period rather than binge. A year-long goal produces no feedback until it is too late to act on; a monthly one forces a measurement and a re-plan twelve times. **Tension it does not resolve:** the same guidance tells users to deploy the skill in the real world as soon as possible, which is exactly the behaviour those users avoid out of fear of mistakes. The most useful behaviour is the one least likely to be chosen unprompted. Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-to-learn-a-new-skill (Duolingo blog, 2024-01-01; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

- **Set two or three concrete can-do goals, plus at most one marked stretch.** More than one stretch and the marking stops meaning anything.
- **Mark it visibly and in advance**, in the same place the other goals live. The label is the whole mechanism.
- **Back-plan from a chosen stopping point**, not forward from available effort. Pick the finish line, then divide it into a per-day quantity small enough to be boring.
- **Keep the horizon at weeks.** Short enough to re-plan against, long enough to show movement.
- **Distribute the work.** Massed effort feels productive but skips the effortful retrieval after partial decay that creates durable memory.
- **Report the ceiling honestly when the goal is met.** Claiming more than the goal covered destroys the credibility of the next goal you set.

## Apply to your product

- If a user told you what they wanted from your product this month, could your product turn that into two scorable goals plus one stretch — or does it only offer usage targets?
- What is the smallest daily quantity that reaches your users' realistic one-month outcome, and do you ever show them that number?
- Where does your product currently present an ambitious outcome without marking it as ambitious, so that missing it reads as failing?
