# Ship An Easier Variant For People Who Failed Before

## Concept

A user who has failed at your domain before arrives carrying a self-assessment, and that assessment suppresses effort regardless of their actual ability. You cannot argue it away, and you cannot out-teach it, because they will not put in the effort that good teaching requires.

What does move it is a win. An artificially regular, low-exception version of the domain strips out the sources of early failure so the user accumulates concrete evidence against their own verdict. The point of that variant is not the content it delivers. The point is the win.

## What Duolingo does

- Duolingo positions Esperanto as a deliberate on-ramp rather than a curiosity. The properties it advertises are all failure-source removals — **regular spelling**, **no grammatical gender**, **uniform conjugation** (no irregular verbs to memorize), and vocabulary shared across European languages so much of it is already half-known.
- It describes the result as the language-learning experience with **fewer grammatical headaches**, and reports that many Esperanto learners describe **more success and more confidence** — **especially those who had previously struggled** with other languages. That last clause is the whole design: the variant is targeted at the population carrying the failed self-assessment, not at beginners generally.
- The course is positioned as a real product with real completion, not a demo or a tutorial mode. The user finishes something.
- Source: blog.duolingo.com/why-learn-a-made-up-language (Duolingo blog, 2021-05-11; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **The honest limit.** The skill transferred out of an artificially regular domain is partial — the exceptions the variant removed are real in the full domain, and the user will meet them later. Duolingo accepts that because a user who never re-attempts learns nothing at all. The trade is coverage for re-entry.
- **The other risk.** If the easy variant is visibly labelled as the easy variant, it can confirm the verdict instead of disproving it. Duolingo sidesteps this by giving Esperanto its own identity and reasons to exist, so taking it is a choice rather than a demotion.

## The transferable pattern

- **Segment on prior failure, not on skill level.** A first-timer and a returner who failed need different products even at identical measured ability. The returner needs evidence; the first-timer needs instruction.
- **Build the variant by removing exception classes**, not by shortening the path. Regularity is what prevents the early failure; brevity just gets them to the same wall faster.
- **Let them finish it.** A completable easy version produces a completion. A trimmed-down sampler produces nothing to point at.
- **Do not label it as remedial.** Give the variant its own reason to exist so choosing it is not an admission. A path badged "easy mode" re-states the verdict you were trying to overwrite.
- **Be explicit that the win is the deliverable.** Judge this surface by re-attempt rate on the real thing, not by how much of the domain it covered.

## Apply to your product

- Who among your users has tried your category before and quit, and does your product currently treat them any differently from a first-timer?
- If you removed every exception, edge case and special rule from your core flow, what would be left — and is that a shippable thing someone could complete in a sitting?
- What would tell you this variant worked — and is that metric about the variant, or about what users did on the real product afterwards?
