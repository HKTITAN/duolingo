# Set Success At Functional Adequacy

## Concept

If your product implies a perfection standard, every attempt becomes a test the user expects to fail, so they defer attempting. Redefining success as *functionally adequate* makes the very first attempt a win, and wins are what generate the volume of practice that real competence requires.

The second half of the move is that the fallback has to be **sanctioned**. Users will not deploy an unofficial workaround under pressure — they suspect it is cheating, or that it will be judged. Naming the degraded path as legitimate is what converts it from a guilty hack into a usable tool.

## What Duolingo does

- Duolingo dismantles its own domain's perfection myths explicitly. Native-sounding pronunciation is **"unrealistic and (thankfully) unnecessary"**. On grammar, **"it's more important to focus on being understood than it is to have everything exactly right."** On vocabulary, nobody knows all the words in their own first language, and describing around a missing word is itself successful communication. **Carve-out it states:** if the user's goal is formal professional output, correctness rules do matter more — functional adequacy is goal-dependent, not universal. Source: blog.duolingo.com/language-learning-myths (Duolingo blog, 2023-03-23; accessed 2026-09-22)
- The sanctioned fallback is written out as instruction, not implied. **"If you don't know how to say something, try phrasing it a different way. Instead of 'overjoyed,' just be 'very happy.' Instead of 'fascinating,' try 'interesting.'"** The same substitution is framed as a feature elsewhere — "maybe instead of 'strawberry,' you have to say 'my favorite red fruit'" — and argued to beat a dictionary lookup, because working around the gap **"forces you to strengthen the connections you've already started building."** Source: blog.duolingo.com/tips-to-improve-writing-skills (Duolingo blog, 2022-03-08; accessed 2026-09-22)
- Coverage beating correctness is demonstrated with a real inventory. Cindy Blanco reports an Italy trip taken without the past tense, substituting the "have seen / have eaten" form instead — **"I didn't always use it correctly, but it always got my point across."** Her entire working kit was **3 phrases** — *È possibile?*, *Andiamo a ___*, *senza ___* — and it carried most of a trip after a few months of study. **"I used them constantly!"** Source: blog.duolingo.com/learn-language-for-trip (Duolingo blog, 2023-04-19; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **The unresolved edge.** Duolingo claims here that the degraded path is *better* than the correct one — working around a gap beats looking the answer up. That only holds while the goal is skill-building. If the goal were precise output, the ranking reverses, and the corpus does not say which mode a product should default to.

## The transferable pattern

A user mid-task cannot pause to acquire the correct form. A worse construction they can deploy now succeeds; a better one they cannot retrieve fails completely.

1. **State the success bar in outcome terms** — "the other side understood you", "the report was usable" — not in correctness terms.
2. **Give them a small kit that covers many situations imperfectly** rather than a large one that covers each situation exactly. Three tools used constantly beat thirty recalled never.
3. **Name the degraded path in your own voice**, in the product, with an example. "Instead of X, just do Y." Unofficial is not the same as sanctioned.
4. **Teach routing around a gap, not filling it.** Composing from what the user already holds strengthens those pieces; a lookup bypasses the retrieval that builds them.
5. **Declare the carve-out.** Where output is formal, public or contractual, correctness does dominate. Say which mode each surface is in rather than letting users guess.

## Apply to your product

- What is your product's implicit definition of a correct result, and what is the cheaper result that would still have been worth the user's time?
- Which workaround do your experienced users already rely on, and have you ever endorsed it in the interface?
- Which of your surfaces is a practice surface and which produces output someone else reads — and does your feedback behave differently across that line?
