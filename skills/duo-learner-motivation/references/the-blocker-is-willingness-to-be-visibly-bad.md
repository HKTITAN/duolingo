# The Blocker Is Willingness To Be Visibly Bad

## Concept

When users tell you they are constitutionally unable to do the thing your product teaches, the capacity claim is usually a rationalisation of an avoidance behaviour. The ability is there; the error-producing practice that converts ability into skill is what is missing, because being wrong feels expensive.

This matters because the two diagnoses lead to opposite builds. If you believe the capacity story, you build better explanation. If you believe the avoidance story, you build cheaper failure. Arguing the belief away does nothing. Making the error cost near zero does.

## What Duolingo does

- Duolingo's treatment of the critical period concedes the hard part first — childhood acquisition genuinely is easier — then reframes the window as one of **relative ease rather than of possibility**. Researchers place the end of the second-language critical period somewhere between ages **7 and 13**, and there may be multiple critical periods, including an earlier one for a first language. Neural plasticity persists into adulthood, and learning itself promotes it.
- It then enumerates the actual adult-versus-child differences — exposure, need, willingness to make mistakes, access to explicit instruction, real-world experience, goal specificity — and identifies **mistake-avoidance as the adult disadvantage**, while listing explicit learning and real-world context as adult *advantages*. The deficit is behavioural, and it is the only one on the list you can design around. Source: blog.duolingo.com/critical-period-learning (Duolingo blog, 2026-04-14; accessed 2026-09-22)
- Video Call is the mechanism built on that diagnosis. The AI partner **never corrects grammar**, applies **no penalty** for mistakes, pauses as though thinking, adapts down when the user says "can you repeat that" or "I don't understand", and lets one wrong word pass as long as the point lands. Conversations run about **a minute** early in a course and up to **3 minutes** as the user advances; the character's signature sarcasm is introduced progressively rather than all at once. Source: blog.duolingo.com/video-call (Duolingo blog, 2024-09-24; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **The trade is deliberate and costly.** The user finishes the exercise with uncorrected errors. Duolingo buys volume of attempts with short-term accuracy feedback, on the bet that volume is what builds the skill.
- Duolingo hedges that bet elsewhere by moving the correction rather than deleting it — missed items are revisited **at the end of the same lesson**, so the user sees the item again and fixes it while the correct answer is still retrievable. Source: blog.duolingo.com/are-some-people-better-at-learning-languages (Duolingo blog, 2023-07-25; accessed 2026-09-22)

## The transferable pattern

If users avoid your core action, stop debating whether they are capable and start pricing the failure.

- **Separate the two diagnoses explicitly.** Ask: has this user tried and failed, or not tried? Not-trying is an avoidance problem and better explanation will not touch it.
- **Suppress correction during the attempt; move it after.** Interrupting mid-attempt teaches users to optimize for not being wrong, which means doing less. Re-surfacing the same error at the end of the session keeps the information and drops the interruption.
- **Make the audience zero.** Cheap failure and private failure are two different levers, and private is usually the stronger one.
- **Make the system visibly imperfect too.** A counterpart that hesitates, adapts down on request, and has a personality lowers the implied standard more than any reassuring copy does.
- **Say the cost out loud when you choose this.** You are trading immediate correctness for attempt volume. That is right when the bottleneck is participation and wrong when the output ships to someone else.

## Apply to your product

- Where does your product interrupt a user mid-attempt to tell them they are wrong, and what would break if that feedback moved to the end of the session instead?
- Which of your failure states is visible to other users, and does it need to be?
- For your most-avoided feature, do you actually know whether non-users tried it and failed, or never opened it — and does your instrumentation distinguish those?
