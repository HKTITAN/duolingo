# Declassify The Perceived Prerequisite

## Concept

Most users who have stalled are holding one specific belief about a gate. They think there is a capability they must possess before they are allowed to attempt the real thing. A perceived prerequisite stops attempts entirely, and stopped attempts produce zero progress regardless of how good your instruction is.

The fix is not to teach the gating feature harder. It is to find the gate, name it, and rule it non-blocking in a sentence. The capability then improves as a side effect of use rather than as a precondition for it.

## What Duolingo does

- Duolingo identifies the exact thing users fixate on and shoots it down by name. On the French "r" — the sound learners believe marks them as unqualified — it rules that **"any 'r' will do — an English 'r,' a rolled Spanish 'r' — all pronunciations are understandable. So, don't let a charming accent on your 'r' sound keep you from speaking with confidence!"** It then hands over a deliberately cheap approximation rather than the correct articulation — say the English word "hose", tighten the throat slightly, and you arrive at "rose". Source: blog.duolingo.com/how-to-pronounce-french (Duolingo blog, 2025-08-26; accessed 2026-09-22)
- It attacks the deferral pattern with equal directness. Against the "wait until I'm ready to speak" myth, Duolingo's position is **"You can benefit from speaking on day 1"**, and it reframes the task as practising what you already hold in simpler sentences rather than waiting to finish a unit or clear a vocabulary threshold. Source: blog.duolingo.com/language-learning-myths (Duolingo blog, 2023-03-23; accessed 2026-09-22)
- The scaffolding it pairs with that permission is a script. A learner spending a week using only French in Quebec wrote and **"shamelessly used" scripts before every phone call**, and was coached by a Duolingo learning scientist to drop expectations for perfection — rely on what you already know, do more describing, pointing and repeating, on the grounds that hardly any lifelong bilinguals communicate the same way in both their languages. She had built a **100-day streak** in preparation, and the experiment ran **one week**. Duolingo names "speak from day one" and "excel at making mistakes" as the actual differentiators of fast learners. Source: blog.duolingo.com/french-for-a-week (Duolingo blog, 2026-02-05; accessed 2026-09-22)
- **The limit of the move.** Declassifying the gate restores the attempt; it does not supply the skill. The Quebec week still required a streak's worth of preparation and produced plenty of failure. Permission is the unlock, not the work.

## The transferable pattern

Avoidance is self-reinforcing: while the user waits, the fear never gets disconfirmed and the skill never accumulates, so the perceived gap widens. Meanwhile the milestone they are waiting for is usually arbitrary — no state of readiness arrives on its own.

- **Go find the gate.** It is one specific thing, users will name it unprompted in interviews, and it is almost never the thing your onboarding emphasizes.
- **Rule it out in one explicit sentence**, placed where the hesitation happens. An implicit "you probably don't need this" is not trusted; a stated permission is.
- **Ship a cheap approximation of the gating capability**, not the correct version. Good-enough now beats correct later, because later does not arrive.
- **Do not defer the scariest core action to a later milestone.** Every day it is postponed it grows, and the user builds an identity in which that action is something other people do.
- **Pair permission with a script.** The first unsupported attempt carries retrieval load, performance anxiety and fear of judgement at once. A prepared script removes the retrieval load so only the attempt remains.

## Apply to your product

- What is the one capability your hesitant users believe they need before they are allowed to use your core feature, and where in your product do you say they don't?
- Which action does your onboarding postpone to "later", and is that milestone real or just the shape of the tutorial?
- What is the cheapest version of your scary action that would still count as having done it — and can a user reach it in their first session?
