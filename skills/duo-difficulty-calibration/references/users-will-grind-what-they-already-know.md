# Users Will Grind What They Already Know

## Concept

Given a free choice, users spend a striking share of their time repeating things they can already do. It is not irrational. Repeating mastered material produces high accuracy, fast completion, visible reward and a clean sense of competence. It produces almost no gain. Fluency during a task is the signal that nothing new is being encoded — which means the user's own in-the-moment satisfaction is anti-correlated with their progress.

This is the one case where "follow the engagement signal" actively hurts. Any system that optimizes toward what users choose, enjoy and complete will drift toward comfortable repetition and stay there, because comfortable repetition wins on every short-horizon metric. The product has to push in the opposite direction from the preference, and it has to do so explicitly, because the user cannot feel the difference from inside the session.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/whats-the-best-way-to-learn-with-duolingo (Duolingo blog, 2025-12-02; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo tells learners to spend **most of their time on new content in the main path**, not in the personalized Practice Hub — and names the urge to practice until "perfection" as the trap.
- The case it makes for pushing forward: **5 sections of Duolingo is equivalent to roughly 5 semesters** of university instruction, with results visible in **as little as five weeks**.
- **The tension, in Duolingo's own product.** This sits directly against the streak, which rewards a **three-minute daily minimum** — and the same company publishes a list of **23 tips** for hitting that minimum. The post has to tell learners in so many words that keeping the streak alive is not the same as making progress. The habit mechanic and the learning mechanic point different directions, and Duolingo does not pretend otherwise.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/why-are-conversations-in-other-languages-so-hard (Duolingo blog, 2024-09-03; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Stated flatly: "do new lessons and don't only review old ones." The pressure to know material perfectly before moving on is called **"both unrealistic and not actually good for making progress."**

Source: blog.duolingo.com/review-exercises-help-measure-learner-recall (Duolingo blog, 2021-12-02; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Only the first, most basic tier of each unit was required in order to progress. Duolingo's own measurement found learners who did the **next tier up** were **significantly more likely to recall that content later** — so the optional depth was where the durable gain lived, and most users were skipping it.

## The transferable pattern

Assume the comfortable path is the default path, and build against it.

1. **Do not let repetition be the cheapest way to satisfy your headline commitment.** If a daily goal, a completion counter or a reward can be satisfied by redoing solved work, most of it will be.
2. **Separate "kept the habit" from "made progress" in the interface.** These are two different achievements. A product that renders only the first teaches users to optimize the first.
3. **Make the forward path the default surface, and the repeat path a deliberate detour.** Not removed — deliberate. The point is which one you land on when you do nothing.
4. **Check whether your optional depth is where the value is.** If the tier most users skip is the tier that produces retained capability, "available" is not enough — it needs to be visible and rewarded. See [[opt-in-hard-mode-priced-higher]].
5. **Accept the cost honestly.** Pushing users off comfortable repetition will lower per-session satisfaction. That trade is defensible; pretending it does not exist is not.

## Apply to your product

- What can a user do in your product that feels productive and changes nothing? How much of your total usage is that?
- Can your daily goal, streak or completion metric be satisfied entirely by re-doing solved work? What would it take to require one genuinely new thing?
- Which is the default surface when a user opens your product cold — the one that moves them forward, or the one that feels good?
