# Intensity and frequency are two axes

## Concept

How much a user does per visit and how often they come back are independent variables. Collapsing them into one engagement score produces a number that can sit perfectly flat while the underlying population inverts, and it hides the two cohorts you most need to see separately: the person who shows up every single day and does almost nothing, and the person who does an enormous amount and then disappears for three weeks. Those two are not "medium engaged users." They are two different problems whose fixes point in opposite directions, and an average of them describes nobody.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/global-language-report-2020 (Duolingo blog, 2020-12-15; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo scores countries on **two separate rankings**, never merged: **"hardest-working"** (lessons completed per learner — intensity) and **"most dedicated"** (longest streaks — frequency).
- The two rankings disagree, which is the point. **Germany ranked #1 hardest-working**, edging out Japan. **Hungary ranked #1 most dedicated, Japan #2, Germany #3.** Japan and Germany score high on both axes; most countries do not.
- Duolingo gives the off-diagonal cells **names rather than leaving them as residue**: **"dabblers"** — very dedicated, a little at a time — and **"binge-learners"** — many lessons, in shorter bursts.
- Naming them is what makes them actionable. A cell with a name gets an owner, a hypothesis and an intervention; a cell that only exists as a low composite score gets a generic re-engagement email.

## The transferable pattern

- **Publish two numbers, not one.** Volume per active session, and rate of return. Refuse to average them — a composite is only defensible when its inputs correlate, and if they correlated you would not need both.
- **The high-frequency, low-depth user has the habit and is missing the value.** They have already solved the hard problem: you occupy a slot in their day. The intervention is to make the slot worth more — deeper defaults, a next step surfaced inside the session, a reason to stay past the minimum.
- **The high-depth, low-frequency user has the value and is missing the habit.** They get real benefit when they show up; nothing reminds them to. The intervention is a return cue and a reason for today, not more content.
- **Give each off-diagonal cell an internal name.** Unnamed segments do not get staffed.
- **Watch the composite for silent inversion.** If your engagement score is flat quarter over quarter, check whether the mix underneath it has swapped; a stable average is compatible with a total population change.
- Cost of the split, stated plainly: two rankings create two possible headlines and two ways for a team to claim a win. The tidy single score buys that control by deleting the finding.

## Apply to your product

- Take your engagement metric. What are its two biggest inputs, and could they ever move in opposite directions?
- Who are your frequent-but-shallow users, and is anything in the product trying to deepen their session rather than just congratulate them for arriving?
- Who are your deep-but-rare users, and do they have any cue at all that today is a day to come back?
