# Name the Capability Gained at the End

## Concept

Whatever progress you display is the thing users will optimise. If forward motion through a map is the only legible reward, people take the route that produces the most forward motion — which is rarely the route that produces the most capability. The depth work loses, not because users do not want it, but because choosing it looks, on your own progress display, like doing less.

The counterweight is cheap. At the end of a unit, say specifically what the user can now do that they could not do before. That activates the outcome they actually came for, which is a stronger pull than the extrinsic progress signal you built — strong enough that people will voluntarily choose the harder path over the faster-looking one.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-difficult-exercises (Duolingo blog, 2021-09-24; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo added **level-up cards that explain how the learner's proficiency improved during that level** — early levels build recognition of new material, later levels force recall, speaking and pronunciation rather than re-presenting what was already shown.
- The measured result was that learners **levelled up more frequently and sought out more advanced exercises**. Telling people what the harder work buys made them choose the harder work.
- **The tension is in the diagnosis.** The problem being fixed was that learners were focused on moving down the course rather than on levelling up — Duolingo's own progress visualisation was steering people away from the deeper practice. The card is a patch on an incentive the map created.
- A related post makes the long-horizon version of the argument — over months and years, **motivation and access to material matter as much as intrinsic difficulty**. Its concrete inversion is that someone living in Seoul may get better and more plentiful Korean input than Spanish input, even though Spanish is on average the easier target for an English speaker. The closing advice is duration-shaped rather than rate-shaped, a little at a time for a long time (blog.duolingo.com/whats-the-easiest-language-to-learn (Duolingo blog, 2026-08-20; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- When some users want more difficulty than your main product should carry, Duolingo's answer is a **side channel rather than a higher floor**. Duo's Film Club, a podcast season built around **six iconic Spanish-language films**, was described openly as more advanced than usual seasons, for learners wanting an extra challenge, with the lineup curated partly from collected learner suggestions (blog.duolingo.com/duos-film-club-podcast (Duolingo blog, 2024-01-09; accessed 2026-09-22)).

## The transferable pattern

- At every completion boundary, answer one question in the user's own terms — what can you now do that you could not do an hour ago? Not what you finished, what you gained.
- Before adding any progress visual, ask what behaviour it rewards when a user is choosing between two paths. Whatever the display makes look like progress is the path most people will take.
- Total outcome is roughly rate multiplied by duration, and **duration has the larger dynamic range because it can go to zero**. Steer people toward the harder thing they care about over the easier thing they do not — an easy path abandoned in week three returns nothing.
- Serve your most advanced users through a **parallel optional surface**, not by raising the difficulty of the main product. Raising the floor makes the front door harder for a much larger beginner segment; a side channel is encountered only by the people who went looking for it.
- Source that harder surface from what those users already asked for. They will tell you, and it costs nothing to collect.

## Apply to your product

- Take your last completion screen. Does it state an accomplishment of yours (unit finished, points earned) or a capability of theirs?
- Where do your users have a choice between a fast-looking path and a valuable one, and which does your interface currently reward?
- Who are your most advanced users, what do they want that would break your onboarding if you shipped it in the main flow, and where could it live instead?
