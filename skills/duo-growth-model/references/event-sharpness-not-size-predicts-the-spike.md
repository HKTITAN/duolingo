# Event Sharpness, Not Size, Predicts the Spike

## Concept

External shocks create demand you did not have to manufacture, but the size of the resulting spike is governed by the sharpness of the trigger rather than its magnitude. A sudden, unambiguous change sends an entire population to the same decision point at the same instant, and the demand arrives as one wave. A staggered or ambiguous version of the identical total change spreads the same behaviour over weeks and flattens it below the level where it reads as a spike at all. The practical consequence is that your response should be timed to clarity, market by market, not to the size of the underlying event.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/changes-in-duolingo-usage-during-the-covid-19-pandemic (Duolingo blog, 2020-04-08; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo published new-user growth by country against the timing and style of each government's stay-at-home orders. **New users doubled over March 2020 (+101%)**, with **+39% week over week from March 9–16**.
- The contrast is the finding. Versus end of February: **U.S. +66%** — blunted, the post notes, by state-by-state guidelines — against **U.K. +296%**, arriving a week later after sudden uniform guidance. Also **France +107%, Spain +109%, Argentina +292%, India +108% then past +320%, Mexico +82%, Chile +91%, Peru +92%, Brazil +92%**; **China +456%** versus end of January, following its February 20 isolation measures.
- The same pattern held over a longer window: **30M new learners March 11 – April 30 2020, +67% year over year** — **more than 2x the 2020 New Year spike and 1.5x the 2019 one**. **Iraq +640%** versus 2019; **China still +300%** weeks after its lockdown; and **Brazil produced multiple smaller spikes** rather than one, as its protocols stayed unclear (blog.duolingo.com/global-language-report-2020 (Duolingo blog, 2020-12-15; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- Tension: these are observational country comparisons with no control, and the countries differ in far more than policy style. The mechanism is inferred from the shape of the curves, and the magnitudes are not transferable to a different category. What transfers is the ranking — decisive trigger, one big wave; ambiguous trigger, several small ones.

## The transferable pattern

- **Rank incoming shocks by sharpness, not headline size.** Ask whether the change breaks everyone's routine at the same moment or drifts in over weeks. The first produces a wave you can plan a launch around; the second produces a slope you will mistake for normal growth.
- **Watch the announcement, not the underlying condition.** Demand follows the moment people are told their situation has definitively changed, which can lag or lead the actual change considerably.
- **Stagger your response by market.** If the trigger lands at different times and with different clarity in each region, a single global launch date wastes the sharp markets and arrives early in the diffuse ones.
- **Read a multi-spike pattern as a signal about the trigger, not a failure of your campaign.** Fragmented demand usually means the population never got a single clear cue, and no amount of spend will consolidate it.
- **A shock-driven cohort is not a strategy.** It is borrowed demand with unusually low habit strength. The spike is worth catching only if the mechanism that holds it is already in place.
- **Do not bake a shock cohort into your baseline.** A wave this size distorts every year-over-year comparison that follows it, so mark the window in your reporting or you will spend the next year explaining a decline that is really a return to trend.

## Apply to your product

- What external event in the next year could break your users' routines — and would it land as one uniform announcement or as a staggered rollout?
- Do you monitor the announcements that govern your users' routines per market, or only your own aggregate signup line?
- If a sharp shock doubled your arrivals next month, what is already built that would keep them, and what would you be scrambling to ship during the wave?
