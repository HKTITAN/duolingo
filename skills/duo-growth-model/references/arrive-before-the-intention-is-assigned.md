# Arrive Before the Intention Is Assigned

## Concept

There is a narrow window in which someone has decided to change something but has not yet decided what they will do about it. In that window your product can become the answer. After it closes, the intention has been attached to a specific plan and you are arguing someone out of a commitment they just made. Land on the occasion itself and you have a different problem: the occasion is consuming the day, and the person is least reachable exactly when their intent is highest. So the timing target is not the event. It is the few days before it, while the intention is still unassigned.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/which-countries-are-the-best-at-keeping-their-new-years-resolutions (Duolingo blog, 2016-03-31; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The New Year campaign went **into full force on December 29** — days ahead of the occasion — with **more than 60 million newsletters sent in 15 languages** against a user base of about **120 million** at the time.
- The resulting curve is the whole argument. Both new sign-ups and sessions by existing users show a **clear peak on December 30**, a **drop on December 31**, and then a **recovery from January 1** that stayed high.
- December 31 is the trough, not the peak, despite being the day the resolution is nominally made. The day of the occasion is the day the occasion wins.
- The cohort acquired in that pre-occasion window was also the more durable one: it stayed more active than the typical 2015 cohort in every country studied, out to **60 days** — so early arrival did not buy low-quality volume.
- Tension worth carrying: this is a single natural experiment on one annual event in one category, not a controlled test of send date. The dip could be a send-volume artifact or a general holiday effect. The shape is suggestive; treat the exact date as a hypothesis to test in your own calendar, not a constant.

## The transferable pattern

- **Find the formation window, not the event.** For any recurring occasion in your category, the intent starts forming days before and gets assigned to a specific action shortly after. Your campaign belongs in the gap.
- **Assume the day itself is your worst slot.** Attention on the occasion is committed to the occasion. High intent and low availability coincide.
- **Being first is being the default.** An unassigned intention needs a vessel; the option present at that moment usually becomes it, which is cheaper than displacing whatever got there first.
- **Instrument the shape, not the total.** Plot arrivals by day across the window rather than summing the period, or you will average the peak and the trough into a flat number that teaches you nothing about when to send.
- **This is a timing rule, not a volume rule.** Arriving early with nothing to hold the cohort just moves the churn forward. Pair it with the retention mechanic.

## Apply to your product

- What is the recurring moment in your category when people decide to change something — a fiscal year, a quarter close, a move, a semester, a season?
- Do your arrivals peak before that moment or on it? If you have never plotted the daily curve across the window, you do not know.
- If you moved your next campaign three days earlier, what would you need in place to hold the cohort that arrives with high intent and no habit yet?
