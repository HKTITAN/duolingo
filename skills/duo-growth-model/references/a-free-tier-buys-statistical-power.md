# A Free Tier Buys Statistical Power

## Concept

Price is the main throttle on scale, and scale is the main input to two things: how much behaviour you can observe, and how small an effect your experiments can detect. At low volume most tests are underpowered, so you ship on opinion and call it judgment. Remove the price throttle and the observation volume becomes an asset no paying-only competitor can match — which reframes the free tier as research and development spend rather than a marketing giveaway. The cost is real and permanent: once the core is free, everything you sell has to sit adjacent to it rather than gating it.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingos-values-a-letter-from-the-ceo (Duolingo blog, 2020-04-15; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Luis von Ahn names the **two best decisions** the company made: give the product away free, which allowed rapid scaling and unprecedented data on how people learn; and make it fun, because the hardest part is staying motivated. At the time of writing: **250 employees, >95% employee retention**, most downloaded education app in App Store history, top-grossing education app worldwide, **all learning content 100% free**.
- The flywheel is stated explicitly in the company strategy: more users feed the experimentation engine → better product → better outcomes and engagement → organic word of mouth → more users → more subscribers → funds more innovation. Growth investment is "almost all in product R&D, with marketing used to strategically accelerate growth."
- Scale as of Q1 2025: **~117M MAU, ~40M DAU (10x since 2019), 9.5M subscribers**, paid share of MAU up from **~5% at the 2021 IPO to 8.8%**, **850+ employees** (blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-company-strategy (Duolingo blog, 2025-04-07; accessed 2026-09-22)).
- Tension, stated in the CEO letter's own terms: keeping the core permanently free is a hard constraint on what can ever be charged for. Every paid offering must be adjacent — convenience, capacity, status — never the thing itself. That closes off the simplest monetization path forever, in exchange for the volume.

## The transferable pattern

- **Compute your minimum detectable effect at current volume.** If the smallest effect you can detect is larger than the effects you actually ship, your experiment program is theatre and more traffic is the fix, not better statistics.
- **Score free access as R&D, not CAC.** The return line is "effects we can now detect and behaviour we can now observe", and it belongs in the research budget where it competes with tooling, not in marketing where it competes with ads.
- **Decide what free permanently forecloses, in writing, before you commit.** If the core is free, the revenue model must be adjacent to it. Teams that discover this two years in end up charging for the core anyway and paying in trust.
- **Volume compounds into quality, which is what the paying minority is buying.** The free users are not subsidized by the payers; they are what makes the paid thing worth paying for.
- **Watch the paid share as a diagnostic, not a target.** A slowly rising conversion rate on a fast-growing base means the adjacent offering is keeping up; a flat one means you are buying observations you are not converting into anything.
- **This does not transfer to every business.** Where marginal cost per user is high, or where the valuable observations come from a handful of large accounts rather than from many small ones, volume buys you much less and the calculation flips.

## Apply to your product

- At your current traffic, what is the smallest lift your standard experiment can detect? How many of last quarter's shipped changes were actually that big?
- If access were free tomorrow, what would you sell instead — and can you name it now, or are you hoping to find out later?
- Which of your product decisions in the last quarter were settled by data, and which by the most senior opinion in the room because the test would never have reached significance?
