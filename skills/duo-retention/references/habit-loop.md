# Habit Loop

## Concept

A habit loop is four beats: a **trigger** that prompts behavior, an **action** the user takes, a **variable reward** that makes them want to repeat it, and an **investment** that increases the cost of leaving. (Frame popularized by Nir Eyal's *Hooked*; Duolingo is the canonical product example.)

The investment beat is the load-bearing one: it's what turns a fun product into a sticky one.

## What Duolingo does

- **Trigger:** evening push notification, often character-driven ([[../duo-voice/references/push-notification-copy]]).
- **Action:** a 3–5 minute lesson, scoped so completion is achievable in any spare moment.
- **Variable reward:** XP earned, league movement, occasional treasure chests, character reactions ([[variable-reward]]).
- **Investment:** streak day, league standing, completed unit on the path. All of these *belong to the user* and would be lost on churn.

## The transferable pattern

For any product trying to become a habit, name each beat explicitly:

| Beat | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| Trigger | What pulls the user in today, and is it the user's idea or yours? |
| Action | Can it be done in under five minutes? If no, you have a project, not a habit. |
| Variable reward | Is the payoff predictable enough to expect, unpredictable enough to chase? |
| Investment | What does the user accumulate that they would lose by leaving? |

Most teams skip Investment. That's why most products aren't habits.

## Apply to your product

- Map your product onto these four beats, literally. If any beat is empty, it's the next thing to build.
- Which beat is weakest? Strengthening that beat is usually the highest-leverage retention work you can do.
- Is your "Investment" actually a cost of leaving, or just a sunk cost the user can ignore?
