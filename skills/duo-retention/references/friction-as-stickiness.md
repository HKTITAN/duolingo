# Friction as Stickiness

## Concept

The reflex in product is to remove friction wherever it appears. But friction is the mechanism by which users *invest* in the product. A streak is friction. A mistake budget is friction. Choosing a learning path is friction. Removing all of it produces a product that's easy to use and easy to forget.

The discipline is: remove friction from the *first session*, add deliberate friction to the *long-term* relationship.

## What Duolingo does

- The first lesson is fast, low-stakes, and immediately rewarding — onboarding friction is minimal.
- Once the user is invested, the product *adds* friction: a depletable resource that limits mistakes, units that require completion before unlocking, daily quests that require return.
- The streak itself is friction made desirable — the requirement to come back is the feature.

But Duolingo also shows where the calibration goes wrong, and fixed it. Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-energy (Duolingo blog, 2025-07-03; accessed 2026-09-22) — under the old **Hearts** system each mistake cost a heart, and beginners were **2x more likely to run out of hearts mid-lesson**. That is friction landing on exactly the cohort with nothing yet invested. In July 2025 Duolingo replaced Hearts with **Energy**, a battery you spend on lessons and *earn back by getting answers right*, with reviewing your mistakes costing nothing. Same mechanic class, inverted incidence: the friction now scales with failure instead of punishing the first attempt ([[../duo-gamification/references/hearts-and-energy]]).

The symmetric move is worth noting: friction can be added to the *alternative* rather than removed from yours. Duolingo's own advice to distracted learners is to put obstacles in front of rival apps — "Putting obstacles between yourself and the distractions can make your higher priorities more appealing" — while conceding the awkward part: "Phones are so distracting. But also, Duolingo is on your phone" (blog.duolingo.com/adhd-study-tricks (Duolingo blog, 2022-03-15; accessed 2026-09-22)).

## The transferable pattern

A useful frame:

| Phase | Friction strategy |
|---|---|
| First-time user | Remove every barrier to first value. Frictionlessness wins. |
| Returning user, week 1 | Introduce small commitments — a goal, a streak, a path. |
| Long-term user | Keep meaningful friction; it's the source of sticky behavior. |

Anti-patterns:
- Adding onboarding friction (long signup forms, tutorial walls) — kills first-session retention.
- Removing all friction for long-term users — they have nothing to be invested in.
- Friction as paywall — extracts revenue, doesn't build habit.

## Apply to your product

- Is your first session frictionless? If a user can't reach value in 60 seconds, that's the bug.
- Does your *returning* user have anything they're invested in — a streak, a saved state, a profile, a status?
- Where have you removed friction that was actually doing useful work? (Test: did engagement drop after the change?)
