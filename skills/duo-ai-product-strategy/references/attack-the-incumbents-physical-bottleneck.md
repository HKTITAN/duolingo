# Attack the Incumbent's Physical Bottleneck

## Concept

Some incumbents are expensive for a reason that looks like physics but is actually a consequence of scarcity. If the artifact you hand a user is drawn from a small fixed set, that set leaks, and the only defense left is controlling the room the user sits in. The room brings travel, appointments, staff, scheduling and a price floor with it.

Generation breaks the chain at its first link. Assemble every session uniquely from a bank large enough that prior knowledge of the items is worthless, and the guarantee now rests on unpredictability rather than on supervision. Everything the supervision was paying for becomes removable. The catch is that a guarantee you removed still has to be supplied from somewhere, or buyers will not accept your output.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/duolingos-most-popular-course-turned-high-stakes-test (Duolingo blog, 2021-07-21; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The Duolingo English Test is a computer-adaptive exam whose item bank is machine-generated with AI and NLP. It began as a **24-hour hackathon in 2013** — a yes/no real-words-versus-pseudowords vocabulary task, made probabilistic and adaptive — then a free "Test Center" alpha in **2014** and the DET proper in **2016**. Duolingo describes it as the first high-stakes test using ML end-to-end at every step.
- The incumbent cost structure it attacks: **$200+** plus travel and lodging, a week-plus wait for scores, and an extra fee to send them. The DET is **$65**, taken from home, on demand. By **2021** it was accepted by **3,000+ universities and programs** and taken by hundreds of thousands of people across **207+ countries**.
- The removed guarantee gets rebuilt in two ways. Human-in-the-loop: expert proctors and test designers work alongside the models so the result still meets the industry's security standards (blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-technology-innovations (Duolingo blog, 2022-06-13; accessed 2026-09-22); **3,500+** institutions accepting by then).
- And a written standard: in **March 2023** Duolingo published responsible-AI standards for high-stakes assessment covering accountability, transparency, fairness, privacy and security, explicitly because the field's most recent standards date from **2014** and predate modern AI (blog.duolingo.com/ai-improves-education (Duolingo blog, 2023-03-30; accessed 2026-09-22)). It then filed a detailed response to the UK Department for Education and gave evidence in parliament (blog.duolingo.com/ai-democratize-education (Duolingo blog, 2023-09-07; accessed 2026-09-22)).

**Two tensions Duolingo names itself.** It is lowering the cost of a gatekeeping barrier it did not create and does not remove — the post says English's status as gatekeeper is intertwined with a long history of imperialism and injustice. And on the standards side, it concedes the fear is not baseless, that bad actors exist and policymakers have real concerns to address.

## The transferable pattern

1. Ask what the incumbent's guarantee actually rests on. If it rests on the artifact being scarce, generation is not a feature you are adding — it is a solvent for their whole cost structure.
2. Trace the chain outward. One removable constraint usually drags a dozen costs with it (premises, staffing, scheduling, wait time, distribution fees). That chain, not the model, is the advantage.
3. Name the guarantee you deleted and re-supply it deliberately — unpredictability, audited human oversight, a published standard someone can check you against. Where your field's written norms predate your technology, publishing the first credible standard makes your framing the thing competitors and regulators argue with.
4. Say out loud which part of the incumbent's harm you are inheriting. Cheaper access to a gate is not the same as removing the gate.

## Apply to your product

- What is the physical or procedural constraint your incumbent's pricing quietly depends on, and is it there because their content is finite?
- If you dissolved it tomorrow, what guarantee would your buyers lose — and what would you put in its place that an auditor could inspect?
- Which governing standard in your market was written before your technology existed, and what would it cost you to publish a concrete one first?
