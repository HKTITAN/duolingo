# Bind The Cue To A Person, Place Or Time

## Concept

Intention has to be regenerated on every single occasion, and on every occasion it competes with whatever else is present. A cue that is already reliably in the environment fires the behaviour without a decision, so adherence stops depending on how the user feels that day. The binding has to be to something outside the user — a specific person, a specific room, a specific slot in the day. "When I get a chance" is not a cue; it is the absence of one.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/tips-for-teaching-kids-your-mother-language (Duolingo blog, 2025-02-18; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo describes two family strategies for keeping a second language alive at home, and both are bindings to something external rather than resolutions.
- **One person, one language (OPOL)** — each parent is permanently bound to one language. The cue is a person. Nobody has to decide anything; who is in the room determines the behaviour.
- **Context binding** — "Inside the house, we speak Portuguese," or "On the weekend, we speak Arabic." The cue is a place or a time window with a hard edge.
- In both cases the rule is stated once and then enforced by the environment, not re-chosen daily.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/language-learning-myths (Duolingo blog, 2023-03-23; accessed 2026-09-22)

- For adults, Duolingo identifies the real constraint as **opportunity, not brain plasticity** — the standard "adults can't learn languages" claim is the myth being rebutted.
- The prescriptions follow from that — study at the same time each day, explicitly "like with your morning coffee," and attach practice to an existing hobby, such as a target-language playlist for the next workout, rather than carving out new time.
- The claim underneath is worth stating plainly — for a time-poor person, attachment to a fixed daily ritual predicts survival of the behaviour better than motivation does.

## The transferable pattern

Design the recurrence around a cue that already exists in the user's environment, and make the binding explicit.

- **Person, place, or time — pick one and name it.** The three categories of cue that are reliably present without the user arranging them.
- **Set the binding once, during setup.** Ask "when" and "where" at onboarding and store the answer, instead of asking the user to remember later.
- **Prefer hard edges.** "On weekends" and "in this room" fire cleanly. "Regularly" and "when I have time" never fire at all.
- **Do not bind to a feeling or an intention.** Motivation is the thing the cue is supposed to make unnecessary.
- **Bind the ritual, then let the reminder confirm it** — a notification that arrives at a slot the user chose is a confirmation; one that arrives at a slot you chose is an interruption.

## Apply to your product

- Does your product know when and where each user intends to use it, or does it only know when they last did?
- Which of your recurring behaviours could be attached to a fixed slot or a specific place the user already occupies?
- If a user told you their cue during setup, what would you change about when and how you contact them?
