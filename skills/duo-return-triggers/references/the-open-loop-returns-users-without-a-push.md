# The Open Loop Returns Users Without A Push

## Concept

A self-contained unit of content or work has to re-earn attention from scratch every time it ends. An unresolved one does not — it leaves a question open, and the pull to come back and settle it is generated inside the user rather than pushed at them. That matters economically as well as psychologically. Every return you get from an open loop is a return you did not spend a notification, an email, or an ad impression to buy, and notification budget is the most inelastic resource in any recurring product.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/true-crime-meets-spanish-learning (Duolingo blog, 2020-12-03; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The Duolingo Spanish Podcast had run for years as anthology seasons — **eight episodes, a different storyteller each week**, each episode complete in itself.
- For **Season 9** they inverted the format. "El gran robo argentino" is **one true-crime story told across six weekly episodes**, each ending on a cliffhanger. First episode published **22 October 2020**.
- The theory was stated up front and is a retention theory, not an editorial one — hooked listeners tune in week after week, and more weeks of exposure means better listening comprehension. The open loop delivers the core value as a side effect of the story.
- It **broke Duolingo's record for most downloads of any podcast episode ever**, with **hundreds of thousands of downloads**, and drew coverage in The Verge, Marketplace and Vulture.
- Worth noting what the format costs. A serial has to be commissioned and produced as a whole before the first part ships, it cannot be reordered, and a listener who joins at part four has a worse first experience than an anthology listener would. The lift is real and so is the production rigidity.

## The transferable pattern

Structure some of what you ship so that finishing one unit opens the next.

- **Find the natural ending you can move.** Wherever your product currently resolves cleanly — a report, a module, a level, a weekly digest — there is an option to end one beat earlier.
- **The loop must be genuine.** A cliffhanger that resolves into nothing is a bait, and users only fall for it once. The unresolved thing has to be something they actually want settled.
- **Count the returns it buys.** Compare return rate on serialized content against your push-driven return rate, and price the difference in messages you did not have to send.
- **Keep an entry point for latecomers.** Serial structure raises the cost of joining midway; a short recap or a standalone opener limits the damage.
- **Do not serialize everything.** Anthology units acquire new users; serial units retain existing ones. They are different jobs.

## Apply to your product

- Where does your product currently end cleanly, and what would it cost to end one step before the resolution instead?
- What proportion of your weekly returns are bought with a message, and what would it mean if a tenth of them were self-generated?
- If someone arrived at part four of your best sequence, would they stay?
