# Bounded Windows Create A Return Date

## Concept

Something available at all times stops being an event, and an event is what people show up for. With no edges there is nothing to mark the moment to engage, so the offer sinks into the background exactly in proportion to how reliably it is there. A bounded window does three things a permanent one cannot — it creates anticipation before it opens, urgency while it is open, and absence after it closes. And if the window recurs on a fixed date, it installs a return trigger that costs nothing to maintain, because the calendar does the reminding.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/spargelzeit-german-asparagus-season (Duolingo blog, 2023-07-18; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo documents German food seasons as a live cultural mechanic rather than trivia. **Spargelzeit** — white asparagus season — begins in **mid-April** and ends abruptly on Johannistag, **June 24**. Stands and festivals appear and vanish on those dates.
- The scarcity is socially enforced, not merely agricultural. Beelitz's asparagus festival draws **30,000 people** and crowns a Spargelkoenigin. **Germany is the world's 4th largest asparagus producer.**
- It is one slot in a recurring annual calendar of **at least 4 named food seasons** — Pfifferlingszeit, Pflaumenzeit (**August to September**), Federweisser-Zeit, Sauregurkenzeit — so the closing of one window is the approach of the next.
- Duolingo's stated read is the transferable part — modern refrigeration means we are no longer even aware of when produce is in season, and it is the bounded season, not the product, that restores the appreciation.
- The cost is real. A bounded window means deliberately refusing revenue and usage outside it, and it only builds anticipation once the calendar has repeated enough times for people to expect it. The first year of a season is just a launch.

## The transferable pattern

Take something you currently offer permanently and give it edges.

1. **Hard dates at both ends.** A start people can count down to and an end that actually arrives. A soft close teaches everyone that the window is decorative.
2. **Make it recur on the same date.** The second cycle is what converts a campaign into a calendar, and the calendar is the return trigger.
3. **Let the absence do work.** The gap is not dead time; it is what makes the reopening an occasion. Resist filling it with a permanent lesser version.
4. **Chain the windows.** If one closing points at the next opening, you have a year-round rhythm made of scarce parts.
5. **Only bound what can survive being missed.** Core functionality should never go behind a season. Bound the special thing, not the thing people depend on.

## Apply to your product

- What do you offer year-round that nobody notices, and would it be noticed if it were only available for six weeks?
- Is there a date your users could learn to expect something from you, repeated until they expect it without being told?
- What would you lose in the closed weeks, and is that less than what you currently lose to indifference?
