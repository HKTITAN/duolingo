# Re-Entry Resumes, It Does Not Restart

## Concept

What a user built before they left was weakened by disuse, not erased. The structure is still there; only the retrieval path has decayed. That makes reactivation dramatically cheaper than acquisition — but only if the re-entry is designed to reactivate rather than to rebuild. Restarting a returning user from zero both wastes a latent asset and quietly insults them, and the restart itself is often what causes the second churn. The related trap is the proficiency check. Testing someone at the moment they return confronts them with exactly how much they lost, which is the one thing capable of pushing them straight back out.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/what-is-memory (Duolingo blog, 2023-01-24; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo's learning scientists invoke the **savings paradigm** — memory traces persist as a network of connections that disuse makes hard to retrieve but does not unravel, so relearning runs far faster than first learning.
- The illustration is concrete — an author with roughly **7 years of childhood piano, abandoned for nearly 30 years**, progressed through relearning much faster than a true beginner would.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/relearning-family-language-essay (Duolingo blog, 2022-12-13; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Akbar spoke fluent Urdu at about **one year old**, lost it entirely, and found the phonology intact in high school Spanish class — "while the other kids struggled with phrases like día de los muertos, I could easily parrot the teacher's pronunciation," because the two languages share the same d and t distinctions. "Without even realizing it, I was reproducing a sound I had made many times before."
- Tension the essay makes unavoidable — retention was **partial and lopsided**. The sounds came back for free; grammar, vocabulary and the writing system did not. Reactivation is real but uneven across components, so do not promise a returning user that everything will come flooding back.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/back-to-school-study-tips (Duolingo blog, 2023-08-01; accessed 2026-09-22)

- The re-entry prescription is **5 tips, all zero-assessment** — watch TV in the language, read a book below your level (explicitly, do not worry if it is below your reading level), sing along to music, draw your favourite silly sentences, and sleep. Every one is framed as jogging memory rather than learning.

## The transferable pattern

- **Resume from the stored state.** Reopen the last project, the saved draft, the completed progress — not the first-run tour.
- **Open with something easy and already half-known.** The first session back should produce a success, not a measurement.
- **Defer any evaluation.** If you need to know where they are, infer it from a few low-stakes actions rather than asking them to sit a test.
- **Be honest about uneven decay.** Some of what they had is intact and some is genuinely gone; say which parts you will help rebuild.
- **Price the offer accurately.** "Pick up where you left off" is both the cheaper promise and the truer one.

## Apply to your product

- What does a user who has been gone 60 days see on their first screen back, and is any of it a test?
- What state do you still hold for them that you are not showing them?
- Which parts of their prior competence decay fastest in your product, and does your re-entry rebuild those specifically?
