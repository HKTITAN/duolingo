# Piggyback An Existing High-Frequency Trigger

## Concept

The scarce resource in a daily habit is not motivation, it is the trigger. A new behaviour with no cue has to be remembered, and remembering competes with everything else in the moment. A behaviour attached to a cue that already fires dozens of times a day inherits an occasion for free, in contexts nobody had to schedule. This is why "set a reminder" underperforms "move the thing into the path they already walk" — muscle memory around where an icon sits is stronger than an intention to open it.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/tips-for-maintaining-streak (Duolingo blog, 2022-12-19; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Among **23 published streak tips**, the structural ones are all trigger relocations rather than motivation appeals — physically swap the app icon's home-screen position with a constantly-opened app such as a social feed or email, add the home-screen widget so the current state is visible without a decision to check, set the web version as the browser homepage, and bundle a lesson onto coffee-making or a morning walk.
- The list includes a deliberate backstop — an alarm at **11:50pm** labelled "Did you do your lesson?" — a cue that fires only when every earlier cue has already failed.

Source: blog.duolingo.com/study-tips-from-learners (Duolingo blog, 2023-03-07; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Learner-supplied tips of the same shape — a family builds images of word groups and sets them as phone wallpaper "so we see the words whenever we pick up the phone," and another trains a short-video feed's language settings so the feed itself delivers target-language content.
- Duolingo's own commentary names the mechanic — these tips "help you incorporate language practice into your regular habits—even including picking up your phone."
- The daily commitment Duolingo asks for is small enough to fit inside a borrowed occasion — framed elsewhere as **15 minutes of daily practice**.

## The transferable pattern

Instead of manufacturing a new occasion, find the highest-frequency thing your user already does and put your entry point inside it.

1. **Inventory their existing triggers.** Unlocking a phone, opening a chat client, the first browser tab of the day, a standing meeting, a commute.
2. **Relocate rather than remind.** Change where your surface lives — default tab, pinned sidebar item, browser start page, home-screen position, an embed in the tool they already keep open.
3. **Size the action to the borrowed slot.** A piggybacked occasion is short. If your minimum useful session does not fit in it, shrink the session before you fight for the slot.
4. **Keep one low-frequency backstop** for the days every borrowed cue misses — but treat it as a net, not the plan.
5. This is placement work, not content work. It costs you nothing in your core product and it is usually the cheapest retention lever available.

## Apply to your product

- What does your user open by reflex every single day, and could your entry point live inside it instead of beside it?
- What is the smallest genuinely useful session in your product, and does it fit in the ninety seconds someone has while waiting for coffee?
- Which of your current re-engagement spend is buying a new occasion that a relocation would give you for free?
