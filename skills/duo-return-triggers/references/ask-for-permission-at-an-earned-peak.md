# Ask For Permission At An Earned Peak

## Concept

Permission is granted in proportion to perceived value at the moment of asking. At first launch the user has no evidence you are worth being interrupted for, so the ask is a pure cost with a hypothetical benefit. Right after a win, or right at the moment of a loss, the benefit is concrete and self-evident — the same sentence converts far better because the situation has already made the argument. Most products ask once, at the worst possible moment, and then treat the resulting opt-in rate as a fact about their users rather than a fact about their timing.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/lessons-from-asia-turning-local-research-into-global-experiments (Duolingo blog, 2021-02-02; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Local research surfaced a measurable gap — **learners in Japan were 50% less likely to opt into reminder notifications** than the global average.
- Duolingo treats notifications as **one of the biggest factors affecting whether a learner forms a habit**, so that gap was a retention problem, not a settings problem.
- The response was not one better prompt. They ran experiments that (a) moved the opt-in prompt to more effective locations, (b) changed the pitch, and (c) added the prompt in entirely new contexts.
- The two new contexts are both emotional peaks, in opposite directions — **after a leaderboard promotion** (an earned win worth protecting) and **when a learner runs out of Hearts** (a felt loss where a reminder is visibly useful).
- The finding started as a regional one and was turned into global experiments. A segment-specific deficit is often a general weakness that the average was hiding.

## The transferable pattern

Treat the permission ask as three independent variables, then place it where the user has just generated their own reason to say yes.

1. **Placement.** Which screen, after which event. Move it off the cold-start sequence entirely.
2. **Pitch.** What the ask claims it will do for them, in their words, not the platform's.
3. **Context.** How many distinct moments carry a version of the ask. One is almost never right.

The two reliable peaks are the moment something valuable has just been earned, and the moment something has just been lost or run out. Both make "we will tell you when this matters" self-evidently useful. A flat rate across a cohort is a signal you have a placement problem, not a preference problem.

Do not ask again immediately after a denial — most platforms only give you one real prompt, so spend it at a peak.

## Apply to your product

- What is the single best thing that has ever happened to a user inside your product, and is your permission ask anywhere near it?
- Where do your users hit a wall, run out, or lose something — and does anything offer to warn them next time?
- Is any segment opting in at half your average rate, and have you checked whether the fix raises the average for everyone?
