# New Content Beats A Guilt Nudge

## Concept

A lapsed user already knows they stopped. A nudge that points at the absence only restates something they feel bad about, and asks them to return to exactly the thing they already declined. An announcement of something genuinely new changes the offer instead of repeating the ask, and it gives the user a reason to come back that is not about their own failure. Shipping is therefore a reactivation channel, and a launch announcement should be planned and measured as one — not filed under marketing and forgotten by the retention team.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/back-from-the-brink-what-duolingo-learned-about-its-resurrected-users (Duolingo blog, 2017-08-30; accessed 2026-09-22)

- Duolingo's baseline is stable and known — **resurrected users, defined as returning after 30 or more days inactive, run at about 5% of daily active users**.
- On the day Duolingo emailed a newsletter announcing the **launch of the Japanese course**, that figure **jumped from 5% to 8% of DAU** — the lapsed cohort grew by more than half in a single day, from one send.
- The message was an announcement of something that did not exist before, not a reminder of an unfinished streak or an unused account.
- The same post shows why this cohort is worth the send — resurrected users are around **20% less likely than a new user to still be active at 7 and 14 days**, so they are cheap to attract and expensive to keep. The announcement gets them in; it does not by itself keep them.

## The transferable pattern

Treat every real launch as a scheduled reactivation event.

- **Announce a change in the offer, not a change in their behaviour.** "This exists now" outperforms "you have not been here."
- **It has to be genuinely new.** A cosmetic release announced as a reason to return burns the channel for the next real one.
- **Segment the send to the lapsed cohort explicitly** and measure the lapsed-user spike as a named outcome of the launch, alongside adoption by current users.
- **Pair the spike with the re-entry path.** An announcement that lands a returning user on a flow built for people who never left converts a win into a second churn. The arrival is the cheap part.
- **Budget launches across the year partly for this.** If your only reactivation lever is a nudge, your calendar of new things is also your calendar of returns.

## Apply to your product

- When you last shipped something significant, did anyone measure how many lapsed accounts came back that week?
- Does your win-back messaging talk about what changed in the product, or about what the user stopped doing?
- If you sent one email to everyone who left six months ago, is there anything true and new enough to put in it?
