# Threat Copy

## Concept

Threat copy is the deliberately-funny variant of [[push-notification-copy]] — copy that *threatens* the user with the loss of the streak, the consequence of inaction, or Duo's mock-disappointment. The genre became a meme because Duolingo did three things right: the underlying product is wholesome (so the threat reads as comic), the threats are exaggerated to absurdity, and the user is in on the joke.

This is the most-imitated and most-misunderstood part of the brand.

## What Duolingo does

- The classic format: Duo expressing disappointment about missed practice, in language that escalates into absurdity.
- Threats are about loss of progress (streak, league, etc.) — they map to a real consequence the user has elected to care about.
- They never threaten anything outside the product. No fake urgency about offers, no FOMO manufactured from thin air.
- The character matters: Duo can threaten because Duo is also goofy. A faceless brand making the same threats reads as hostile.

## The transferable pattern

Threat copy works when:

1. **The threat is about something the user genuinely values losing.** Without [[../duo-retention/references/loss-aversion|loss aversion]] underneath, it's just rude.
2. **The escalation is comic.** "We noticed you missed today" is dry. "Duo has been sitting outside your house for six hours" is in on the joke.
3. **The brand can carry the menace.** A character can threaten; an abstract logo cannot.
4. **The threat is rare.** Daily threats become a tax. Threats spaced and surprise-driven become a story.

Threat copy fails when it's used for things the user didn't sign up to care about, when the menace is real instead of comic, or when the brand isn't established enough to support the joke.

## Apply to your product

- Is there anything in your product that the user *would* feel real loss about losing? That's the only place threat copy could go.
- If you wrote one threatening notification, would it read as funny or hostile? What's the test?
- Is your brand strong enough — recognized character, established voice — to support the menace?
