# In-Group Vocabulary

## Concept

The function of in-group vocabulary is to mark separation from everyone else. That means adoption by an out-group does not spread the word, it destroys the exact property that made the word worth using — and the originators abandon it. A brand reaching for a community's current vocabulary is not joining that community; it is ending that term's usefulness to it, usually just late enough that the audience can see the lag.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/slang-all-languages (Duolingo blog, 2025-11-11; accessed 2026-09-22)

Duolingo's linguistics team documents the lifecycle directly: a term loses its in-group status as soon as it spreads beyond that group and starts appearing in the mouths of politicians and companies. The tell is self-referential — **mainstream explanation of the term is itself evidence the term is over**, which is a problem for anyone whose plan was to explain it.

- **Institutional recognition is a lagging indicator.** German publishers name a "youth word of the year" annually, and it is usually already outdated by the time it is announced.
- **The words that survive stop being markers and become ordinary vocabulary.** "Tip" was slang in the early 18th century; "booze" dates to the 14th. Neither signals membership in anything now.

**Tension.** Duolingo's own social accounts trade heavily in exactly this register, and the post analysing the lifecycle is published by the same brand. That is not hypocrisy so much as the real shape of the rule: the constraint is about timing and standing. Where the brand is a participant with its own long-running in-jokes, borrowed vocabulary works; where it is an institution arriving late with a glossary, it does not.

## The transferable pattern

1. **Coin rather than borrow.** Terms you invent for your own users are yours to keep and cost the originating community nothing.
2. **Never explain the borrowed term.** If your copy has to gloss it, you are addressing the out-group, and the in-group can tell.
3. **Treat any term that has reached an official list as expired** for brand use — the list is the announcement that it has left the in-group.
4. **Ask who you are to this audience.** A participant with standing can use the vocabulary; an institution using it reads as costume.

## Apply to your product

- Which words in your marketing did you borrow from your users rather than invent?
- Does your copy ever explain a term it is also trying to use casually?
- What running in-joke is genuinely yours — one your users would recognise as originating with you?
