# Unmarked Framing

## Concept

Framing carries a message the words never state. Calling a trait out as notable announces that it departs from the default; describing something as "how X differs from the normal way" installs a hierarchy in which your reference point is correct and everything else is a curiosity. Both moves reinforce exactly what you were trying to dissolve, and the second one makes the unfamiliar thing feel harder to adopt than it is. The fix is cheap: let it appear incidentally, and describe differences in both directions.

## What Duolingo does

Source: blog.duolingo.com/lgbtq-representation-in-duolingo-stories-and-characters (Duolingo blog, 2021-06-15; accessed 2026-09-22) · blog.duolingo.com/welcome-zulu-to-the-language-family (Duolingo blog, 2022-08-26; accessed 2026-09-22)

- **Depict, don't remark.** Duolingo's three established queer characters — Lin, Bea and Oscar — are defined by traits unrelated to their sexuality: Lin's chronic lateness, Bea's over-planning, Oscar's snobbery. In Stories, a woman mentions her wife during a taxi conversation about her honeymoon and the scene simply continues. Nothing in the copy flags the fact as a fact.
- **Invert the default before you list differences.** The Zulu launch post names the risk up front — the long history of non-Western subjects being presented as curiosities under a colonial frame — then describes the distinctive features symmetrically: English could just as easily be called the odd one out for having so many vowels, or dozens of terms for suburban lawn care.
- **Warn against over-reading your own list.** The same editorial habit appears as an explicit caveat that listed regional features are neither universal within a country nor exclusive to it.

**Tension.** Unmarked framing is quiet by construction, so it earns no credit and is easy to under-invest in; the people it is for may not find it without a marked surface somewhere else. And the pipeline still sets a default: Stories are written in English first and adapted outward, so the reference point is structural even when the prose is symmetrical.

## The transferable pattern

1. **Give every character or example at least two defining traits that have nothing to do with the attribute you are normalizing.** One fact among many reads as ordinary; one fact alone reads as a label.
2. **Never write "unlike the standard way."** Describe both options in the same register, with the same amount of explanation.
3. **When you introduce something unfamiliar, name the framing risk before the feature list** — a single sentence that refuses the hierarchy costs nothing and changes how the list is read.
4. **Audit for the tell:** if your copy explains why something is notable, you have marked it.

## Apply to your product

- Where does your copy describe an option as a deviation from your defaults?
- Do the people or examples in your content carry traits beyond the one you are trying to normalize?
- Which of your defaults are genuinely defaults, and which are just where the work started?
