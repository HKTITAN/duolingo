# Celebration Copy

## Concept

Celebration copy is what the product says when the user wins. The defaults are bad — "Congratulations!" / "You did it!" / "" — and they're bad because they read as auto-generated even when they're not. Real celebration copy is specific, character-driven, and doesn't take itself too seriously.

## What Duolingo does

- Streak milestones, league promotions, and lesson completions each have distinct copy registers — they don't all say "Nice work!"
- Lines reference what the user actually did: "Your 7-day streak is the longest you've had this month." Specificity beats generic praise.
- Some celebrations are deadpan ("Adequate."), some over-the-top ("LEGENDARY."), some character-cameo'd ("Lily is reluctantly impressed.") — register variety prevents fatigue.
- Negative-coded humor is allowed: a celebration can be ironic ("Eddy assumed you'd quit by now. Eddy was wrong.")

## The transferable pattern

Three rules:

1. **Reference what the user did.** "Great job!" works once. After that, name the specific thing.
2. **Vary register across celebrations.** If every win sounds the same, the wins stop landing.
3. **Some characters can be reluctant.** Universally enthusiastic copy reads as a corporate sales meeting. A reluctant compliment from the right character lands harder.

Anti-pattern: tying celebration copy strictly to milestones. Casual wins ("you finished the lesson") deserve copy too.

## Apply to your product

- When a user succeeds at something, what does your product say? Is it specific or generic?
- Do your celebration moments vary in register, or is there one default?
- Could you write a celebration that's *deadpan* and still feel rewarding?
